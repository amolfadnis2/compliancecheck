# ComplianceCheck 90-Day Customer Acquisition Plan: From Free Beta to 10 Paying Customers/Month

## TL;DR
- **Fix the site first, then sell.** Two conversion blockers will waste every rupee of spend until fixed: (1) the DPDP assessment and gratuity calculator demand full PII (name + email + phone) *before* delivering any value, and (2) only 3 of the claimed ~25 blog articles are discoverable — the rest are orphaned with no internal links and no Google footprint. Neither costs money to fix; both must be done in Week 1–2 before Razorpay goes live.
- **The ₹1,000–2,000/month belongs almost entirely in Google Search retargeting + a handful of exact-match long-tail keywords**, not LinkedIn (₹150–650 CPC, median ~₹320 — the most expensive paid channel in India) or broad Meta. Paid is a supporting act; organic — founder-led LinkedIn, CA/CS referral partnerships, and leverage of the existing blogs — must carry ~80% of the load.
- **10 paying customers/month is achievable at roughly 3,000–5,000 targeted visitors/month** given realistic funnel math (2–5% visitor→assessment-start, ~40–50% completion, 10–20% pay on warm/high-intent traffic). Blended average selling price ~₹1,500 means the first milestone is ~₹15,000 MRR — modest, so the constraint is *qualified traffic and trust*, not price.

## Key Findings

### The product is well-built but the funnel leaks at the top and the trust layer is missing
The homepage is clear: strong headline ("Know exactly where your business stands on Indian compliance — in 15 minutes"), transparent per-assessment pricing (₹499–₹2,999), a sensible free-summary-then-pay-to-unlock model, and three genuinely useful free calculators. On-page technical SEO is competent — clean canonicals, `index, follow` directives, OpenGraph, `en_IN` locale, published timestamps. This is a solid foundation most solo founders never reach.

But three problems will burn marketing spend if not fixed first:

1. **Front-loaded PII gate.** The DPDP assessment asks for Full Name, Company, Email, Phone, State, Employee count, Industry and Revenue *before* the user answers a single question or sees any value. The gratuity calculator does the same at 10% progress — despite the homepage advertising calculators as "No signup required. Free forever." This contradiction and friction is the single biggest conversion risk. (The penalty calculator, by contrast, is genuinely no-login — the one low-friction entry point on the site.)
2. **Orphaned blog content.** The `/blog` index links to exactly 3 articles (POSH ICC requirements, DPDP readiness checklist, Labour Codes 2025). The other ~22 have no crawlable path from any linked page — no pagination, no related-posts, no category/tag pages — no cross-links even within articles, and show no Google footprint. The company's main organic asset is effectively invisible.
3. **Zero trust signals site-wide.** No testimonials, no named expert credentials, no security/privacy badges, no company registration, no team bios, and no privacy reassurance adjacent to the PII capture. For a compliance product asking for payment and personal data, this is a serious credibility gap. The footer lists only a Zohomail address (`compliancecheck@zohomail.in`).

### Competitors are expensive, slow, or upmarket — leaving a clear gap
The Indian SME compliance market splits into three layers, none of which occupies ComplianceCheck's niche:
- **Legal-services platforms** (Vakilsearch/Zolvit, IndiaFilings, LegalWiz, RegisterKaro) sell *done-for-you* filings and registrations. GST registration ~₹399–999, incorporation from ~₹999 + govt fees, annual compliance bundles ₹3,499+. They acquire via massive SEO, Google Ads and heavy phone-sales teams. A recurring buyer complaint across all three is being pushed onto an annual compliance plan before the job is done.
- **Enterprise RegTech** (TeamLease RegTech/Avantis, Simpliance) — SaaS compliance tracking trusted by 2,600+ entities and 50,000+ enterprise users, sold via field sales. Far upmarket of a 10–500-employee SME; no self-serve, pay-per-use option.
- **DIY tax platforms** (ClearTax, Tax2Win) and **local CAs** — recurring filing work, not point-in-time gap assessments.

**The gap ComplianceCheck exploits:** nobody offers a cheap, instant, self-serve *diagnostic* — "am I compliant, where are my gaps, what is my penalty exposure" — as a productised pay-per-use report. That is a genuinely differentiated wedge, and the regulatory calendar creates urgency evergreen competitors lack: the four Labour Codes (Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020, OSH Code 2020) took effect **21 November 2025**, replacing 29 central labour laws; the DPDP Rules were notified **13 November 2025** (published in the Gazette 14 November), with the Data Protection Board constituted immediately, the consent-manager framework live from 13 November 2026, and **full substantive compliance with penalties up to ₹250 crore per violation from 13 May 2027**.

### What ₹1,000–2,000/month realistically buys in Indian performance marketing
- **Google Search:** the 2026 national-average CPC is ~₹24, "but this number is nearly meaningless without industry context." Legal and finance advertisers pay 60–125x more per click than local businesses — pure legal-services keywords can exceed ₹3,000 and banking/finance terms run ₹1,500–2,800. On ₹2,000/month, broad compliance head terms would yield only a handful of clicks. The one viable use is **retargeting** (Display CPMs ~₹50) and **a tiny set of exact-match long-tail keywords** where competition — and therefore CPC — stays low.
- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram):** cheap clicks (₹0.50–₹8) but intent-based B2B targeting is weak, and B2B SaaS/IT cost-per-lead is reported at ₹950–2,800. Meta needs ₹300–500/day minimum to exit the learning phase — far above budget. Not viable as a lead engine at ₹2,000/month; usable only for cheap retargeting of warm audiences.
- **LinkedIn Ads:** India CPC averages **₹150–650 (median ~₹320)**, CPM ₹500–3,500, CPL ₹850–2,500, with a practical minimum of ₹4,000–8,000/day per campaign to gather usable data. It is "the most expensive paid channel in India on a cost-per-click basis," costing 5–15x more than Meta. **Confirmed too expensive** — do not run paid LinkedIn. Use LinkedIn *organically* instead.

The verdict: at this budget, paid ads are for **retargeting warm traffic and capturing a trickle of ultra-high-intent search**, not demand generation.

### Organic channels must do the heavy lifting
Founder-led LinkedIn is the single highest-ROI channel for early B2B SaaS in India — individual accounts out-reach company pages 3–5x, and at ComplianceCheck's stage the addressable audience of LinkedIn-active founders/HR managers is small enough for organic posts to reach a meaningful share. CA/CS referral partnerships are the second engine: CAs already serve dozens of SME clients each and actively look for tools; one CA who adopts the platform can send a steady stream of assessments. WhatsApp CA/finance communities exist in abundance (CAclubindia hosts finance WhatsApp communities; hundreds of CA groups are publicly listed), though ICAI's code of ethics restricts CAs from promotional broadcasting — so the play is partnership and genuine value-sharing, not spam.

## Details

### Funnel model to 10 paying customers/month
Two scenarios, blended ASP ~₹1,500 (conservative Indian B2B self-serve benchmarks: visitor→signup 2–5%, best-in-class >11%; pay-to-unlock on a low-commitment micro-purchase behaves like a warm-traffic self-serve conversion):

**Conservative (cold-ish traffic, e.g. broad SEO/social):**
- 5,000 visitors → 3% start an assessment = 150 starts
- 150 starts → 45% complete free summary = ~68 completions
- 68 completions → 15% pay to unlock = **~10 paying customers ✅**

**Optimistic (warm/high-intent traffic, e.g. referral + retargeting + long-tail search):**
- 2,000 visitors → 6% start = 120 starts
- 120 starts → 55% complete = 66 completions
- 66 completions → 18% pay = **~12 paying customers ✅**

The lever that matters most is **traffic quality**, not volume. Referral and high-intent search traffic converts several times better than cold social. That is why the plan front-loads referral partnerships and founder content over paid reach. Note: the pay-to-unlock rate is the biggest unknown — during free beta it is effectively 0%, so the *first real test* is simply switching Razorpay on and watching whether warm users convert at even 8–10%.

### The ₹1,000–2,000/month paid plan (beginner, step-by-step)
**Do not spend a rupee until the Week 1–2 site fixes are live and Razorpay is active.** Then:

**Phase A (Month 1) — Google Search, exact-match long-tail only (~₹1,500):**
1. Create a Google Ads account; set billing (UPI/card accepted, no minimum); choose a "Search" campaign, objective "Website traffic," and **Manual CPC** (not Smart Bidding — too little data to train it).
2. Set location to India (optionally restrict to metros where SMEs cluster), language English + Hindi.
3. Budget: **₹50/day (~₹1,500/month).**
4. Use **exact and phrase match** on 8–12 ultra-specific, high-intent long-tail terms, e.g. `[labour code readiness assessment]`, `[DPDP gap assessment for SME]`, `"POSH compliance check for company"`, `[FSSAI compliance checklist restaurant]`, `[which labour laws apply to my business]`. These map directly to your assessment pages, keeping Quality Score high and CPC low — the deliberate opposite of bidding on expensive head terms like "compliance" or "labour law."
5. Add negative keywords immediately: `free`, `jobs`, `salary`, `course`, `pdf download`, `lawyer near me`.
6. Point each ad to its **matching assessment landing page**, not the homepage.
7. Cap expectations: this is a *learning* budget to discover which intent converts, not a volume play.

**Phase B (Month 2 onward) — add retargeting (~₹500):**
1. Ensure the PostHog/Google/Meta tag fires on all pages (see measurement plan).
2. Run a small Google Display retargeting campaign (or Meta retargeting via the Meta pixel) to people who visited an assessment page but did not pay. CPMs ~₹50 mean ₹500 buys ~10,000 impressions to a warm audience — cheap reinforcement.
3. Creative: single message — "Finish your compliance check — full report from ₹499."

**What to measure before scaling spend:** if a keyword produces an assessment *start* under ~₹150 and any paid conversion, keep it; if it burns ₹300+ with no starts, pause it. Only increase budget past ₹2,000 once a keyword reliably produces paying customers at a cost you are happy with.

### Organic channel playbook, ranked by effort vs. yield

**1. CA/CS referral partnerships — HIGHEST yield, medium effort.**
Recruit 5–10 practising CAs/CSs as referral partners. Each serves dozens of SMEs and needs exactly this diagnostic to open client conversations. Offer a revenue share (e.g. 20–30% per report) or free white-labelled reports they can resell. Approach via LinkedIn DMs and warm intros, not WhatsApp-group spam. ICAI restricts CA *solicitation*, but you referring business to them, and them using a tool for their clients, is fine. This is the closest thing to a repeatable engine at this stage — a known CA client-acquisition pattern is offering a cheap entry service (e.g. FSSAI licence at ₹500–700) then upselling; ComplianceCheck can *be* that cheap entry hook for CAs.

**2. Founder-led LinkedIn — HIGH yield, medium ongoing effort.**
Post 3–5x/week from the founder's personal profile (not a company page — personal accounts out-reach company pages 3–5x). Content = plain-English breakdowns of the regulatory hooks you own: "The 4 Labour Codes went live on 21 Nov 2025 — here are the 6 things every 50-person company must do now," "DPDP penalties reach ₹250 crore per violation and stack — here's who's actually exposed before May 2027." End each with a soft CTA to a free calculator or assessment. Repurpose existing blogs into posts. This seeds both buyer segments (founders/HR) and the partner segment (CAs).

**3. SEO leverage of existing blogs — HIGH yield, LOW effort (once unblocked).**
Your cheapest win. The content already exists; it is just undiscoverable. Fix internal linking and the sitemap (Week 1–2) and these articles begin compounding organic traffic on exactly the timely terms (Labour Codes, DPDP, POSH, FSSAI) that competitors under-serve. Add a related-posts block, a properly paginated/categorised blog index, and internal links from each article to the matching assessment.

**4. WhatsApp / niche communities — MEDIUM yield, low effort.**
Join MSME/founder communities (HeadStart Network ~27k members, Ahmedabad Startup Community ~17.5k, MSME & Startups Forum-Bharat, SaaSBoomi if eligible) and CA finance communities. Share genuinely useful content — the free penalty calculator is the perfect give-first asset — never spam links.

**5. Product Hunt / Peerlist launch — MEDIUM one-off spike, medium effort.**
A single launch generates a traffic burst, backlinks (durable SEO value) and social proof. Peerlist Launchpad (week-long cycle, India-friendly tech audience, timezone-luck-proof) is lower-risk than Product Hunt's 24-hour sprint. Warm up an audience first; schedule Tuesday–Thursday. Treat it as a one-time awareness + backlink event, not a channel.

**6. Cold email — LOWER yield, higher setup effort/risk.**
Legally viable for B2B in India with proper setup: a separate sending domain, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, 3–4 week warm-up, a dedicated tool (Instantly/Smartlead), and a 3-email sequence over 9–15 days. But it is the most technical and reputation-fragile channel for a solo founder. Defer until referral + LinkedIn + SEO are running, then test small (500 verified contacts) targeting HR heads at 50–500-employee firms.

### Pre-flight site fixes (Weeks 1–2, before any spend)
1. **Remove the PII gate before value.** Let users complete the assessment and see the free summary *first*; capture email only at the point of unlocking/emailing the PDF. This alone should materially lift completion.
2. **Fix blog discoverability.** Add a complete, paginated/categorised blog index; add related-posts and internal links between all articles and to matching assessments; verify every article is in `sitemap.xml`; submit the sitemap in Google Search Console.
3. **Add trust signals.** 3–5 testimonials (even from beta users), a line naming the practising CS/labour-law experts behind the checklists, a privacy reassurance next to any data-capture field, a security/privacy note, and basic company/contact details in the footer.
4. **Resolve the calculator contradiction.** Make calculators genuinely no-signup (as advertised) — they are your lowest-friction top-of-funnel and best community-shareable asset.
5. **Confirm Razorpay + PDF delivery + Resend email** work end-to-end with a test purchase.

### Measurement plan (PostHog)
Define these events and build a funnel:
- `assessment_viewed` (assessment landing page)
- `assessment_started` (first question answered)
- `assessment_completed` (free summary shown)
- `unlock_clicked` (clicked pay/unlock)
- `payment_success` (Razorpay callback)
- `report_downloaded` / `report_emailed`

Build a **PostHog funnel** across these six steps, segmented by traffic source (UTM tags on every link — LinkedIn, referral-partner code, Google Ads, blog). Track: drop-off between each step, conversion by source, and cost-per-paying-customer by channel. Watch `assessment_started → completed` for the impact of removing the PII gate, and `completed → payment_success` for true willingness-to-pay once Razorpay is live. Review weekly.

## Recommendations

**Weeks 1–2 (fix, don't spend):** Ship the five pre-flight fixes. Activate Razorpay. Instrument the PostHog funnel + UTMs. Submit sitemap to Search Console. *Benchmark to proceed:* end-to-end test purchase works; all articles indexed within 2 weeks.

**Weeks 3–6 (organic ignition, zero/near-zero spend):** Start founder LinkedIn (3–5 posts/week). DM and sign 3–5 CA/CS referral partners. Repurpose blogs into posts. Share the free penalty calculator in 3–4 communities. *Benchmark:* first 3–5 paying customers, mostly from referral + LinkedIn.

**Weeks 5–8 (turn on paid):** Launch the ₹50/day Google Search long-tail campaign. Monitor cost-per-assessment-start. *Benchmark to keep spending:* at least one keyword produces a paying customer under ~₹500 CAC.

**Weeks 7–10 (amplify):** Add ₹500 retargeting. Do a Peerlist launch. Scale whichever referral partners are producing. *Benchmark:* 6–8 customers/month run-rate.

**Weeks 9–12 (hit the milestone):** Double down on the top 2 channels by CAC. Add a second cohort of referral partners. Test a small (500-contact) cold-email pilot only if the above is stable. *Benchmark:* 10 paying customers in a single month.

**Threshold to change the plan:** if after Week 8 organic + referral is not producing ≥1 customer/week, the problem is trust/positioning, not traffic — pause acquisition and fix the offer (add case studies, sharpen the "penalty exposure" hook, consider leading with the ₹499 entry assessment as the wedge). If paid-search CAC stays above ~₹1,000 with no path down, cut it entirely and reallocate time to referral partnerships.

## Caveats
- **Beta = no real conversion data yet.** Every conversion rate here is benchmark-derived; the pay-to-unlock rate in particular is unknown until Razorpay is live. Treat the first 30 days post-payment-launch as pure discovery.
- **The blog indexing status is partly unverified.** The `sitemap.xml`/`robots.txt` files could not be directly fetched; the finding that ~22 articles are orphaned is inferred from the blog index and the absent search footprint. Verify manually in Google Search Console before assuming SEO leverage exists.
- **Regulatory dates carry minor source conflicts.** The DPDP Rules were notified 13 November 2025 (per MeitY/PIB) and published in the Gazette 14 November 2025 — both dates appear in reputable sources; the enforcement milestone (13 May 2027) and ₹250 crore per-violation penalty ceiling are consistent across all sources.
- **CPC and conversion figures are third-party marketing-blog benchmarks**, not audited data, and vary widely by keyword, city and season. Use them for planning ranges, not precise forecasts.
- **ICAI solicitation rules** constrain how CAs can be involved — structure partnerships as tool-adoption and inbound referral, not paid promotion by the CA.
- **Solo-founder bandwidth is the real constraint.** Running LinkedIn, referrals, SEO, paid and community simultaneously is unrealistic; the phasing above deliberately sequences them so only 1–2 new channels start at once.
