Under the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, women can now be employed on night shifts across a wider range of workplaces, but only with their consent and only if the employer arranges prescribed safety conditions.

## Key facts at a glance

- The **OSH Code** expands eligibility for women to work **night shifts** beyond the older, more restrictive factory-specific rules.
- **Consent** from the woman employee is a core requirement before assigning her to a night shift.
- Employers must arrange **prescribed safety conditions**, commonly understood to include safe transportation and adequate workplace security.
- A woman employee who **does not consent** should not be assigned to night-shift work.
- Applicability can vary by **sector and state**, since specific safety conditions depend on rules still being finalised in many places.
- The OSH Code is in force nationally from **21 November 2025**, with enforcement depending on **state-level rule notification**.

## Why this is a meaningful change

Older labour law, particularly under factory-specific legislation, placed significant restrictions on employing women during night hours, often requiring special government permission or outright prohibiting it in many contexts. This created friction for sectors like IT-enabled services, healthcare, retail, and manufacturing that genuinely need round-the-clock staffing and where many women employees want the flexibility and pay that night shifts can offer.

The OSH Code reframes the issue around consent and safety rather than a blanket restriction, aligning the law more closely with how many workplaces already operate in practice, while still building in protective conditions.

## The two conditions that must both be satisfied

### Consent

Employing a woman on a night shift is conditional on her actual consent to work that shift. This is not a formality - employers should have a documented process for obtaining, recording, and respecting this consent, including a clear path for an employee to decline without facing adverse consequences.

### Safety conditions

Beyond consent, employers are expected to arrange safety measures around the night shift itself. Commonly understood conditions include safe transportation to and from the workplace during night hours, adequate lighting and security at the workplace, and a generally safe working environment. Exact prescribed conditions will be shaped by state rules, so employers should not assume a single national checklist applies uniformly everywhere.

## Comparing the old and new frameworks

| Aspect | Older factory-specific rules | OSH Code framework |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Default position | Often restricted or required special permission | Permitted with consent and safety conditions |
| Consent requirement | Not always a formal, documented requirement | Central requirement for night-shift assignment |
| Safety conditions | Varied, sometimes informally applied | Expected to be formally arranged by employer |
| Sector scope | Concentrated in factory legislation | Extends more broadly across sectors |
| State variation | Significant historical variation | Still varies, pending state rule notification |

## What employers should put in place

### A documented consent process

Build a simple, clear process for obtaining and recording consent from women employees before assigning night shifts, including how an employee can withdraw consent going forward and what happens to their scheduling if they do.

### Safe transportation arrangements

If your business runs night shifts, arrange safe transportation for women employees travelling to and from work during night hours. This is one of the most consistently emphasised safety conditions across state guidance in this area and should be treated as close to non-negotiable regardless of exact state wording.

### Workplace security and facilities

Ensure adequate lighting, security personnel or systems, and basic facilities are in place for night-shift operations, and periodically review these arrangements rather than treating them as a one-time setup.

### State-specific confirmation

Because exact prescribed conditions depend on state rules still being finalised in many places, confirm your specific state's requirements before finalising a night-shift policy, rather than relying solely on the general OSH Code framework.
