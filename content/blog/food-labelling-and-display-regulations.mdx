FSSAI labelling rules govern what must appear on a packaged food product, and separate display rules govern how a food business must show its licence at the premises. Both are commonly overlooked areas that surface during inspections.

## Key facts at a glance

- Packaged food labels must generally show **product name, ingredients, net quantity, manufacturer details, batch number, dates, and FSSAI licence number**.
- **Allergen declarations** are required where applicable to the product category.
- Food businesses must generally **display their FSSAI licence/registration number** at the premises.
- **Health and nutrition claims** on labels must be substantiated and are subject to FSSAI regulation.
- Labelling requirements apply **regardless of sales channel**, including e-commerce and delivery platforms.
- **"Best before" and "expiry date"** are distinct terms with different regulatory meaning depending on product category.
- Enforcement is carried out by **FSSAI and state-level food safety officers** through inspections.

## Why Labelling Compliance Matters

Labelling is one of the most visible and easily checked aspects of FSSAI compliance - a missing or incorrect label can be flagged instantly during an inspection, unlike hygiene practices that require observation over time. It is also directly consumer-facing: incorrect or misleading labels are a common trigger for complaints, which in turn can escalate a business into a higher inspection risk category under the current risk-based inspection model.

## What a Packaged Food Label Must Show

| Element | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Product name | Clear, accurate description of the food item |
| List of ingredients | In descending order of composition, as applicable |
| Net quantity | Weight, volume or count, as applicable to the product |
| Manufacturer/packer details | Name and address of the entity responsible |
| Batch/lot number | For traceability purposes |
| Date of manufacture | When the product was made or packed |
| Best-before/expiry date | Depending on product category |
| FSSAI licence number | The licence number of the manufacturer or packer |
| Nutritional information | Where applicable to the product category |
| Allergen declaration | Where the product contains common allergens |

Exact requirements can vary somewhat by product category, so businesses in specialised categories (infant food, health supplements, etc.) should check category-specific FSSAI labelling rules.

## Display Requirements at the Premises

Separate from packaging labels, food businesses are generally expected to display their FSSAI licence or registration number visibly at their premises - this is one of the first things checked during any inspection, routine or complaint-triggered. Many restaurants also include the licence number on menus as a matter of good practice, even where not strictly separately mandated, since it signals compliance readiness to both customers and inspectors.

## Claims and Marketing Language

Terms like "natural," "healthy," "organic" or specific health claims are not free-form marketing choices under FSSAI rules - they are subject to substantiation requirements. Using such claims without the backing required by regulation is a labelling violation, not just a marketing risk. Businesses should be cautious about claims that go beyond what their product formulation and testing can support.

## Labelling for Delivery and Online Sales

Because delivery-only and cloud kitchen models package food that customers never see in a store setting, it can be tempting to treat packaging labelling as less important. FSSAI requirements apply the same way regardless of sales channel - a product sold exclusively through a delivery app still needs to meet the same labelling standards as one sold over a counter.

## A Practical Labelling Checklist

1. Confirm your **product category's specific labelling requirements** beyond the general list above.
2. Verify **allergen declarations** are accurate and complete for your recipe.
3. Ensure **FSSAI licence number** appears correctly on both packaging and at the premises.
4. Review any **health or nutrition claims** for substantiation before printing packaging.
5. Update labels whenever your **licence category or number changes**.
