Running a hotel or hospitality business in India means holding together food safety, fire safety, liquor licensing, labour law and guest safety compliance at the same time, and missing any one of them can shut down operations overnight. This checklist covers the compliance areas that matter most for hotels, resorts and serviced apartments.

## Key facts at a glance

- Hotels serving food need an **FSSAI licence or registration**, with the category depending on annual turnover.
- A **fire safety NOC** from the state or municipal fire department is required before a hotel can legally operate.
- Serving alcohol requires a **state excise liquor licence**, and rules differ significantly by state.
- **EPF** becomes mandatory once a hotel has **20 or more employees**, at a wage ceiling of **Rs 15,000/month**.
- **ESI** applies once a hotel has **10 or more employees**, on wages up to **Rs 21,000/month**.
- A **POSH Internal Committee** is mandatory for any hotel with **10 or more employees**.
- Hotels must register under the state **Shops and Establishments Act**, which governs working hours and leave.

## Food safety: FSSAI licensing for hotel kitchens and restaurants

Any hotel serving food, whether through a restaurant, room service or banquet kitchen, needs an **FSSAI licence or registration**. Under the 2026 FSSAI amendment rules, the registration threshold (Basic FSSAI Registration) now covers businesses with turnover up to **Rs 1.5 crore**, and the State Licence threshold has been raised to **Rs 50 crore**. Most full-service hotels will fall into the State or Central Licence category once turnover and outlet scale are factored in.

Licences and registrations issued from **1 April 2026** no longer carry a fixed expiry date — they continue until suspended, cancelled or the business closes. Existing licences issued before that date should be checked separately with FSSAI for their specific renewal treatment. Inspections have also moved to a **risk-based model**, which can include third-party audits for larger properties.

## Fire and building safety

A fire NOC is a non-negotiable requirement for hotels because of the high occupancy and overnight-stay risk profile. This typically requires a fire safety audit, adequate fire exits, alarm and sprinkler systems, and periodic renewal tied to the building's occupancy certificate. Requirements are set at the state or municipal level, so a property in Mumbai and one in Jaipur may face different documentation and inspection cycles.

## Liquor and excise licensing

Any hotel bar, banquet service or in-room minibar serving alcohol needs a licence from the **state excise department**. Excise rules cover permitted serving hours, storage limits, staff training in some states, and pricing controls. Excise licensing is entirely state-specific — a hotel chain operating across multiple states needs to track each state's renewal calendar and fee structure separately.

## Labour law compliance for hotel staff

Hotels are labour-intensive and typically employ a mix of permanent staff, contract workers and seasonal hires. Once headcount crosses the relevant thresholds, statutory obligations kick in automatically.

| Requirement | Threshold | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shops and Establishments registration | From first employee, state-specific | All hotel staff |
| EPF | 20+ employees | Staff earning up to Rs 15,000/month wage ceiling |
| ESI | 10+ employees | Staff earning up to Rs 21,000/month wage ceiling |
| POSH Internal Committee | 10+ employees | All workplaces including hotel branches |
| Professional tax | State-specific | Employees in states levying it |

The four **Labour Codes** (Wages, Industrial Relations, Social Security, OSH) came into force nationally on **21 November 2025**, but each state must separately notify its own rules before they are fully operational there. As of mid-2026 most states have not yet fully notified their rules, so hotel HR teams should track their specific state's status rather than assume the new Codes are fully applicable yet.

## POSH and guest-facing workforce safety

Hotels have large, often young and mixed-gender workforces working overnight shifts, which makes **POSH (Prevention of Sexual Harassment)** compliance especially important. An Internal Committee is mandatory once a property has 10 or more employees, and the Supreme Court has been pushing for mandatory ICC registration on the government's SHe-Box portal along with district-wise compliance audits. Hotel groups increasingly face board-level disclosure expectations tied to POSH compliance.

## Signage, weights and measures, and other local permissions

Beyond the core areas above, hotels commonly need: a trade licence from the local municipal corporation, health trade licence, lift licence (for multi-storey properties), swimming pool and spa permissions, signage permits, and legal metrology compliance for any pre-packaged goods sold in-room or at the gift shop. These are typically annual renewals tracked at the state or city level rather than nationally standardised.
