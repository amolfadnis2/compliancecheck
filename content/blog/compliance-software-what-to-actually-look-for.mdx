Good compliance software should tell you exactly which obligations apply to **your** business based on state, headcount and sector, track deadlines reliably, and flag gaps before they become penalties - most tools that fall short do so on one of those three fronts.

## Key facts at a glance

- Compliance requirements vary heavily by **state, headcount, and sector** - generic checklists often miss this.
- Statutory thresholds that matter for software accuracy include the **EPF 20-employee** and **ESI 10-employee** mandatory-coverage marks.
- The **Labour Codes rollout** (national effect from 21 November 2025, state rules still incomplete) means rule sets need **frequent updates**, not one-time setup.
- **POSH ICC obligations trigger at 10 or more employees**, a common area generic payroll tools omit entirely.
- **DPDP's substantive obligations become enforceable 13 May 2027**, making data-protection tracking a growing must-have feature.
- Most compliance software **tracks and flags gaps** rather than **automatically filing** statutory returns - confirm which one a tool actually does.
- A tool's value depends heavily on **update cadence** - stale rule sets can be worse than no tool at all if they create false confidence.

## Start With Coverage, Not Features

The first question to ask any compliance software is not "what does the dashboard look like" but "does it actually know which rules apply to my business." A tool that gives the same generic checklist to a 15-person Bangalore software company and a 60-person Gujarat manufacturing unit is not doing real compliance mapping - it is doing a generic template with a login screen. Look for software that asks about your state, headcount, industry and structure before generating obligations, not after.

## The Core Capabilities That Actually Matter

### Accurate, State-Aware Obligation Mapping

Given how much varies by state - professional tax slabs, Shops and Establishments rules, and now the uneven Labour Codes rollout - software that treats India as one uniform jurisdiction will miss real obligations. Confirm the tool distinguishes state-specific requirements rather than applying a one-size-fits-all central checklist.

### Deadline Tracking With Real Consequences Attached

A calendar of filing dates is only useful if it also explains what happens if you miss one. Good software connects a deadline to its actual consequence (interest, penalty, licence risk) so the urgency is calibrated correctly rather than every item looking equally important.

### Coverage Beyond Payroll

Payroll compliance (PF, ESI, TDS) is the most commonly covered area, but POSH ICC constitution, Shops and Establishments renewals, and increasingly DPDP data-protection obligations are separate, active compliance areas. A tool scoped only to payroll leaves real gaps in POSH and data protection that a business may not even realize are being missed.

### Update Cadence, Not Just Initial Setup

Regulatory change is currently unusually active - the Labour Codes are mid-rollout, DPDP obligations phase in through 2027, and FSSAI rules changed materially in 2026. Ask any vendor directly how often their rule sets are updated and how you would find out about a change that affects you.

## Feature Comparison Checklist

| Capability | Why it matters | Red flag if missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State-specific obligation mapping | Requirements vary significantly by state | Generic national-only checklist |
| Headcount-based threshold logic | PF (20+), ESI (10+), POSH (10+) all trigger at different sizes | Same checklist regardless of headcount |
| Multi-domain coverage | Payroll, POSH, DPDP are all active compliance areas | Payroll-only scope |
| Deadline-to-consequence linkage | Helps prioritize what actually matters | Flat, undifferentiated task list |
| Update cadence transparency | Labour Codes and DPDP rules are actively evolving | Vendor cannot describe update process |
| Filing vs tracking clarity | Sets correct expectations on what the tool actually does | Vague claims of "full automation" |
| Human escalation path | Software cannot represent you before an inspector | No guidance on when to involve a consultant |

## What Good Software Will Not Claim

Be skeptical of any tool claiming to fully replace a labour consultant or lawyer. Legitimate compliance software is upfront that it identifies obligations and tracks status, while representation before an inspector, response to a legal notice, or judgment on a genuinely ambiguous situation still needs a human professional. Tools that overclaim on this point are usually overselling elsewhere too.
