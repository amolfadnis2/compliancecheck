Schools and edtech companies share a compliance thread that most other sectors do not: both handle large volumes of data and safety obligations relating to children, on top of the standard labour and premises requirements every employer faces.

## Key facts at a glance

- A POSH **Internal Committee** is mandatory for any school or edtech company with **10 or more employees**.
- **EPF** applies once staff strength reaches **20 or more employees**, with a **Rs 15,000/month** wage ceiling.
- **ESI** applies once staff strength reaches **10 or more employees**, with a **Rs 21,000/month** wage ceiling.
- DPDP's substantive obligations - including additional consent requirements for processing children's data - become enforceable from **13 May 2027**.
- Fire NOC and periodic fire safety certification are commonly required for school affiliation and renewal by most state boards.
- Penalties for DPDP breaches involving inadequate security safeguards can reach **up to Rs 250 crore** per instance.

## Child safety and premises compliance for schools

Physical schools carry a distinct layer of obligations built around student safety. A **Fire NOC** is typically required before initial affiliation and at renewal, and many boards - state boards as well as CBSE and ICSE - make fire safety, building stability, and sanitation certificates a condition of continued affiliation, not just a one-time formality.

Background verification of staff who interact directly with children - teachers, bus drivers, support staff - is increasingly expected under state child-safety guidelines, even where it is not a single codified national requirement. Schools should also have a documented child protection policy covering reporting channels distinct from the employee-facing POSH mechanism, since POSH covers employee-to-employee harassment, not student safety incidents.

## Labour compliance for school staff

Teaching and non-teaching staff are employees for the purposes of standard labour law, and schools should not assume any blanket exemption.

| Requirement | Threshold | Key detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EPF | 20+ employees | Rs 15,000 wage ceiling; 12% + 12% contribution |
| ESI | 10+ employees | Rs 21,000 wage ceiling; 0.75% employee, 3.25% employer |
| POSH Internal Committee | 10+ employees | Covers all staff, including contract and support staff |
| Shops & Establishments | Applies from day one | Working hours, leave, and registration display |

Many schools also engage contract staff for transport, catering, and housekeeping through third-party vendors, which brings contract labour obligations - such as verifying the contractor's own PF/ESI compliance - onto the school as the principal employer.

## POSH in the education sector

Schools sometimes assume POSH is only relevant to corporate workplaces, but the Act applies squarely to any employer, including schools, once the 10-employee threshold is crossed. The Internal Committee handles complaints between employees; a separate, clearly communicated channel is needed for anything involving students, since that falls outside POSH's scope and typically under child protection law and, where applicable, POCSO-related reporting obligations.

## DPDP: the edtech-specific pressure point

Edtech platforms - whether standalone apps, school management systems, or online tutoring services - collect names, ages, academic performance, attendance, sometimes biometric attendance data, and parental contact details. Under the **DPDP Act, 2023**, this is personal data, and data relating to individuals under 18 is treated with additional care, generally requiring verifiable parental or guardian consent before processing.

While full substantive DPDP obligations become enforceable from **13 May 2027**, edtech companies building or scaling now should treat consent capture, data minimisation, and retention limits as design requirements rather than a later retrofit - rebuilding consent flows across an existing user base is far more expensive than building them in from the start.

## Business registration and tax basics for edtech

An edtech company is a standard commercial entity for company law, GST, and income tax purposes - there is no special regulatory carve-out simply because the product serves students. GST registration applies once turnover crosses the applicable threshold, and online educational content or coaching services may attract GST depending on the exact nature of the service (a distinction that has been the subject of ongoing clarification, so it is worth checking current treatment before pricing).

## Bringing it together

A school's compliance load leans toward premises and child safety (fire NOC, background checks, affiliation renewals) layered on top of standard labour compliance. An edtech company's load leans toward data protection and standard commercial/labour compliance, with less premises exposure if it operates largely online. Both share the same POSH and PF/ESI thresholds once headcount grows.
