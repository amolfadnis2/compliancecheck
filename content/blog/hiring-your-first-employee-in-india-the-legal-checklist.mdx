Hiring your first employee in India triggers fewer mandatory statutory obligations than most founders expect, but a handful of registrations and documentation steps still apply from day one. This checklist separates what is required immediately from what only kicks in as headcount grows.

## Key facts at a glance

- **Shops and Establishments registration** is generally required from the point a business starts operating, not tied to a headcount threshold.
- **EPF** becomes mandatory only once a business has **20 or more employees**, at a wage ceiling of **Rs 15,000/month**.
- **ESI** becomes mandatory only once a business has **10 or more employees** (**1+ for hazardous units**), at a wage ceiling of **Rs 21,000/month**.
- **Professional tax**, where levied by a state, generally applies from the first employee, with no headcount threshold.
- A **POSH Internal Committee** is only mandatory at **10 or more employees**; below that, the District Officer's Local Committee handles complaints.
- **GST registration** is based on business turnover, not employee count, so it does not change with your first hire.
- The four **Labour Codes** are in force nationally since **21 November 2025**, but state rules are still being notified, so first-employer obligations should be checked against your specific state.

## What applies from your very first hire

A few obligations do not wait for headcount to grow - they apply the moment you have one employee on payroll.

- **Shops and Establishments Act registration**, required in most states as soon as the business starts operating with any staff.
- **Professional tax registration**, in states that levy it, with deductions starting from the first salary paid.
- **A written appointment letter or contract**, covering role, compensation, working hours, leave and termination terms - not always a strict legal mandate in every state, but strongly recommended and increasingly expected under evolving labour code standards.
- **TDS on salary**, if the employee's income crosses the taxable threshold, requiring the employer to deduct tax at source and file the relevant returns.

## What changes as you cross specific headcount thresholds

Most of the "heavier" statutory obligations are threshold-triggered rather than immediate, which is the part first-time employers most often get wrong in either direction - either ignoring them too long, or assuming they apply too early.

| Requirement | Threshold | Wage ceiling / notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shops and Establishments | From first employee | State-specific rules |
| Professional tax | From first employee, where state levies it | No headcount threshold |
| ESI | 10+ employees (1+ for hazardous units) | Rs 21,000/month wage ceiling |
| POSH Internal Committee | 10+ employees | Below 10, Local Committee via District Officer handles complaints |
| EPF | 20+ employees | Rs 15,000/month wage ceiling, 12% employee / 12% employer |

## EPF and ESI: why your first hire alone does not trigger them

A common misconception among first-time employers is that EPF or ESI apply the moment you have any staff. In reality, **EPF is mandatory only at 20 or more employees**, and **ESI is mandatory only at 10 or more employees** (with a lower 1-employee threshold for hazardous units). Below these thresholds, both schemes are generally optional - a business can voluntarily opt in, which some do to make the offer more attractive to a candidate, but it is not a legal requirement for a single hire.

## Documentation that protects both sides

Even where not strictly mandated, a clear offer letter and appointment letter reduce dispute risk significantly. At minimum, cover: designation and reporting line, fixed and variable compensation, working hours and applicable leave policy, notice period and termination grounds, and confidentiality or IP assignment terms if the role involves sensitive information. As the Labour Codes phase in operationally state by state, documentation standards for appointment letters are likely to tighten further, so building good habits now avoids a scramble later.

## POSH before you hit 10 employees

Below 10 employees, you are not required to set up an Internal Committee, but employees are still protected under POSH - complaints in that situation go to the **Local Committee** constituted by the District Officer rather than an internal body. It is worth telling new hires this explicitly in onboarding so they know where to go if an issue arises, even while your business is below the ICC threshold.

## Tracking compliance as you scale past your first hire

The practical challenge for most founders is not the first hire itself, it is remembering to re-check obligations as headcount crosses 10 and then 20 employees, since none of these thresholds send an automatic notification. Building a simple internal tracker tied to headcount milestones, rather than relying on memory, is the most reliable way to avoid missing a threshold-triggered requirement.
