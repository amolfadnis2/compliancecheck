A Trade Certificate under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules is what legally allows a dealer to move, test, or deliver an unregistered vehicle on public roads - without it, that same movement is a violation.

## Key facts at a glance

- A **Trade Certificate** permits a dealer to use unregistered vehicles on public roads for specific purposes like delivery, testing and demonstration.
- It is issued by the **Regional Transport Office (RTO)** under the **Central Motor Vehicles Rules (CMVR)**.
- The certificate has a **fixed validity period** and must be renewed before expiry.
- It covers dealer-specific use cases only, **not general or personal use** of unregistered vehicles.
- CMVR obligations extend beyond the certificate to vehicle safety/emissions standards and buyer registration.
- Operating unregistered vehicles on public roads **without** a valid Trade Certificate is a compliance and legal risk for the dealership.

## Why the Trade Certificate exists

Under the Motor Vehicles Act, a vehicle must generally be registered before it is used on any public road. That creates an obvious problem for dealers, who routinely need to move brand-new, unregistered vehicles between showroom, workshop and customer for entirely legitimate business reasons. The Trade Certificate exists to bridge that gap - it is a defined, time-bound exemption that lets a dealer do exactly this, within limits set by the issuing authority.

## What the certificate actually permits

| Permitted use | Not covered |
| --- | --- |
| Moving unregistered stock between dealer premises | General personal use of an unregistered vehicle |
| Demonstration drives for prospective buyers | Long-distance travel unrelated to dealer business |
| Delivery of a purchased vehicle to the buyer's location | Use after certificate expiry or outside its terms |
| Testing a vehicle as part of dealer operations | Use by anyone other than authorised dealer personnel |

Dealers sometimes treat the Trade Certificate as a blanket cover for any unregistered-vehicle movement. It is not - it is scoped to specific, dealer-related purposes, and using it outside that scope defeats the purpose of holding it.

## Application and renewal

The certificate is obtained from the RTO or relevant state transport authority, and dealers typically need to demonstrate they are an authorized dealership for the category of vehicles covered. Because it carries a fixed validity period, renewal has to be tracked proactively - an expired Trade Certificate leaves a dealer in the same position as never having had one, which is easy to miss if renewal dates are not on a compliance calendar.

## CMVR obligations beyond the Trade Certificate

The Trade Certificate is one piece of a broader set of Central Motor Vehicles Rules obligations dealers carry. Vehicles sold need to meet applicable CMVR safety and emissions standards before delivery. Dealers are also generally expected to facilitate proper registration for the buyer rather than leaving vehicles in an unregistered state after sale, and to maintain accurate records of vehicle stock movement, sales, and any test or demonstration use under the Trade Certificate.

## Common gaps dealers should check

The most frequent issues are certificates that have lapsed without renewal, use of the certificate for purposes outside its intended scope, and inconsistent record-keeping of which vehicle was moved under the certificate and for what purpose. Any of these can turn a routine RTO check into a compliance problem, particularly for dealerships handling high vehicle turnover where record discipline slips first.
