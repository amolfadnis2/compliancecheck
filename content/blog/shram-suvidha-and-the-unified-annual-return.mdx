**Shram Suvidha** is the government's single online portal meant to bring labour law filings, registrations and inspections under one system, and the **unified annual return** is the consolidated filing designed to replace the patchwork of separate returns employers currently file across different labour laws.

## Key facts at a glance

- Shram Suvidha is a central government portal for labour law registration, return filing and inspection tracking.
- The unified annual return is meant to replace **multiple separate returns** previously filed under different labour laws with one consolidated filing.
- All four Labour Codes (Wages, Industrial Relations, Social Security, Occupational Safety Health and Working Conditions) came into force nationally on **21 November 2025**.
- Central rules were pre-published in draft form on **30-31 December 2025**, with final central rules expected around **April 2026**.
- Labour is a **concurrent subject**, so each state must notify its own rules before the Codes are fully operational there.
- As of mid-2026, **most states have not yet fully notified** their Labour Code rules.
- Employers should track their specific state's notification status rather than assume nationwide operational effect.

## Why a unified return exists

Before the Labour Codes, an employer could be filing separate annual and periodic returns under the Factories Act, the Contract Labour Act, the Minimum Wages Act and several other laws, each with its own format and deadline. The unified annual return is designed to collapse this into a single filing that captures the same core data (headcount, wages, welfare compliance, safety) once, rather than repeatedly across forms.

This matters operationally more than legally. Even where the underlying compliance obligations do not change, the administrative burden of filing the same information many times over does, and that is the specific inefficiency the unified return targets.

## How Shram Suvidha fits in

The Shram Suvidha portal is the delivery mechanism for this consolidation. It is intended to let an employer register an establishment, view applicable labour law obligations, file returns and track any inspection activity in one place, rather than navigating separate state and central systems for each law.

### What this means day to day for HR

- One portal login and profile for an establishment, instead of multiple department-specific logins.
- A single consolidated annual data submission, instead of separate filings per law.
- Inspection scheduling and outcomes visible in one place.

## Status: in force nationally, uneven state by state

This is the part employers most often get wrong. The Labour Codes are legally in force across India since 21 November 2025. That does not mean every state's compliance machinery, including the unified return process on Shram Suvidha, is fully operational everywhere yet.

| Milestone | Status as of mid-2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Four Labour Codes in force nationally | Yes, since 21 November 2025 |
| Draft central rules pre-published | Yes, 30-31 December 2025 |
| Final central rules | Expected around April 2026 |
| State-level rules notified | Uneven; most states not yet complete |
| Unified annual return operational everywhere | Not yet uniform; depends on state rollout |

Because labour is a concurrent subject under the Constitution, the Codes cannot become fully operational in a state until that state notifies its own implementing rules. Some states move faster than others, so the practical filing obligations an employer faces today still depend heavily on where its establishments are located.

## What employers should actually do now

Keep filing under whatever labour laws currently apply to your establishments in each state, and do not assume the unified return has replaced your existing obligations until your state confirms it has. Track your state's specific notification status rather than treating the national in-force date as the operational date everywhere.
