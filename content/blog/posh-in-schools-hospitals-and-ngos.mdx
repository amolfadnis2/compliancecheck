Schools, hospitals and NGOs are all "workplaces" under the **POSH Act**, and the law applies to them in the same way it applies to any commercial employer. The complication in these sectors is not whether the law applies, it is working out who counts as covered given the mix of employees, contractors, consultants, students, patients and volunteers typically present.

## Key facts at a glance

- The POSH Act's definition of "workplace" is deliberately broad and covers **educational institutions, hospitals, nursing homes, and NGOs**, both organised and unorganised sector.
- The **10-employee threshold** for a mandatory Internal Committee applies the same way here as in any other business.
- Below 10 employees, or for complaints against the employer, cases go to the district's **Local Committee**.
- Hospitals typically must cover **multiple employee categories** in one ICC, doctors, nurses, administrative, and support staff.
- NGOs and unorganised-sector workplaces are **explicitly included**, not exempted, under the Act.
- Schools often layer POSH with **separate child-protection obligations**, since student safety involves different legal frameworks.

## Why these sectors need extra care

Unlike a typical office where "employee" is a straightforward category, schools, hospitals and NGOs often have layered workforces: permanent staff, visiting faculty or consultants, contract and outsourced workers, interns, and volunteers. Getting the ICC's composition and the policy's scope right requires thinking through who is actually working at or connected to the institution, not just who is on the direct payroll.

### Coverage by sector, at a glance

| Sector | Typical covered groups | Common complication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Schools | Teaching and non-teaching staff, administrative staff | Overlap with student-safety/child-protection laws, which are separate |
| Hospitals | Doctors, nurses, paramedical, administrative, support/outsourced staff | Multiple employment types (employed, contracted, visiting consultants) under one ICC |
| NGOs | Programme staff, field staff, volunteers, project consultants | Unorganised or distributed teams, sometimes no fixed single workplace |

## Schools: POSH is not the same as child protection

A school's obligations under POSH cover harassment involving its women employees, teachers and staff, as workplace participants. This is separate from a school's obligations around student safety, which typically involve different legal and regulatory frameworks focused on protecting minors. Many schools maintain two distinct policies: one POSH policy for staff-related workplace harassment, and a separate child-safeguarding policy for student protection. Conflating the two, or assuming one policy covers both, is a common gap.

## Hospitals: one ICC, many employment categories

Hospitals often run on a mix of directly employed staff, contract nursing and support staff from outsourcing agencies, and visiting or empanelled consultants. The Internal Committee, where mandatory, needs a policy and complaint mechanism that clearly addresses which of these categories are covered and how a complaint involving a non-payroll consultant or contract worker gets handled. Given the sensitivity and complexity, hospitals should confirm the exact treatment of non-payroll categories with a compliance professional rather than assume a default answer.

## NGOs: unorganised structures still need a mechanism

NGOs, especially those working with field staff, community volunteers or project-based teams spread across locations, sometimes assume that their informal or distributed structure puts them outside POSH's scope. It does not. The Act specifically extends to the unorganised sector. Where an NGO's team is under 10 people, or highly distributed without a clear single "employer," complaints route to the district Local Committee, but the NGO still needs a written policy and a way for staff and volunteers to know how to raise a complaint.

## Practical steps across all three sectors

1. **Map every category of person working at or for the institution**, employees, contract staff, consultants, volunteers, and decide which are covered by your policy.
2. **Confirm your headcount for ICC purposes**, counting across all locations if the institution operates from more than one site.
3. **Keep the POSH policy separate from any other safeguarding policy**, especially in schools, to avoid gaps or confusion in scope.
4. **Train staff sector-appropriately**, a hospital's shift-based, multi-category workforce needs a different awareness approach than a small NGO's field team.
