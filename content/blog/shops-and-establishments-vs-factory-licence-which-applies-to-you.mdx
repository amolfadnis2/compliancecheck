Whether your business needs a Shops and Establishments registration, a factory licence, or both depends on what actually happens at the premises - not on how the business is described on paper.

## Key facts at a glance

- **Shops and Establishments registration** covers commercial premises such as shops, offices, and service businesses; it is administered at the **state level**.
- A **factory licence** applies where **manufacturing takes place**, generally triggered by a defined worker-and-power threshold.
- A common threshold is **10+ workers with power**, or **20+ workers without power**, though exact figures can vary by state rule.
- Both registrations are **premises-specific**, so a business with multiple locations may need separate registrations per site.
- The **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code** consolidates factory-related safety law, but state-level notification is still catching up as of mid-2026.
- All four Labour Codes came into force **21 November 2025** nationally, but operational effect varies by state until each state notifies its own rules.
- Renewal cycles, fees, and specific thresholds **differ by state**, so the same business type can face different rules in different states.

## What Shops and Establishments registration actually covers

The **Shops and Establishments Act** (a state-level law, with variations in name and detail across states) governs working conditions in commercial premises that are not factories - shops, offices, IT companies, restaurants, and most service businesses. It typically covers working hours, holidays, leave, and basic conditions of employment, and requires registration of the premises with the state labour department.

Almost every commercial business in India, regardless of sector, needs this registration unless a more specific law (like a factory licence) applies instead.

## What a factory licence actually covers

A **factory licence**, historically under the Factories Act and now being folded into the OSH Code framework, applies specifically to premises where a manufacturing process is carried out, and where the headcount and power-usage thresholds are met. Factory licensing is stricter than Shops and Establishments registration because it addresses higher physical risk: machinery safety, working conditions around manufacturing hazards, and stricter inspection regimes.

The key trigger is the nature of the activity (manufacturing) combined with the scale (worker count and power usage), not simply the size of the business in revenue terms.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Feature | Shops and Establishments | Factory Licence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applies to | Shops, offices, service businesses, restaurants | Premises where manufacturing takes place |
| Trigger | Any commercial premises employing staff | Worker count + power usage threshold (commonly 10+ with power, 20+ without) |
| Governing level | State law | Central framework (OSH Code), with state rule notification required |
| Focus | Working hours, holidays, leave, basic conditions | Machinery safety, hazard controls, stricter inspection |
| Renewal | Periodic, state-specific | Periodic, state-specific |
| 2026 status | State-administered, largely business-as-usual | Consolidated under OSH Code; state notification still uneven as of mid-2026 |

## How to tell which one applies to you

Ask two questions: does the premises carry out a manufacturing process, and if so, does it meet the worker-and-power threshold for that state? If the answer to the first question is no, Shops and Establishments registration is almost certainly the relevant requirement. If yes, a factory licence is likely required in addition to, or instead of, Shops and Establishments registration, depending on the state's specific rules.

A business that has both an administrative office and a separate manufacturing unit may need to register each premises under the law that actually applies to it - registration is generally tied to the specific location and activity, not the company as a whole.

## Why this gets missed

Founders sometimes assume that because their business is "small," neither registration is urgent, or they register once under Shops and Establishments and never revisit the question as the business adds a production or assembly function. Since factory licence thresholds are based on worker count and power usage at a specific site, a business can cross into factory licence territory without a formal announcement - it is worth rechecking whenever headcount or the nature of on-site activity changes.
