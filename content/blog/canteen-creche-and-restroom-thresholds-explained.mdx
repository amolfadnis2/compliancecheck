Indian labour law sets specific headcount thresholds that turn canteens, creches and enhanced restroom facilities from "nice to have" into a **legal obligation** - the moment you cross the threshold, you cannot wait to act.

## Key facts at a glance

- A **creche** is generally mandatory once an establishment employs **50 or more employees**, counting men and women together in most current interpretations.
- A **canteen** is typically mandatory in factories with **250 or more workers**, though several states set a lower threshold in their factory rules.
- These thresholds trace back to the **Factories Act 1948** and **Maternity Benefit Act 1961**, both now folded into the **Labour Codes** framework.
- The Code on Social Security, 2020 **retains the 50-employee creche trigger** rather than raising or lowering it.
- Restroom and washroom facilities scale with headcount under most **state Factories Rules** and **Shops and Establishments Acts**, separate from the canteen/creche thresholds.
- All four Labour Codes are **in force nationally since 21 November 2025**, but state-level rules are still being notified through 2026, so practical enforcement varies by state.

## Why these thresholds exist

Canteen, creche and restroom rules exist to make sure basic welfare facilities scale with the size of the workforce rather than being left to employer discretion. A 10-person startup and a 500-worker factory face very different practical needs, so the law ties specific obligations to specific headcounts rather than applying one rule to everyone.

## The canteen threshold

A canteen becomes mandatory in most factories once the workforce reaches **250 or more workers** on any working day, a threshold that originates in the Factories Act 1948 and continues under the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code, 2020. Some states set this threshold lower for specific categories of factories, so always check your state's factory rules rather than relying on the central figure alone.

The obligation is not just about having a room - inspectors typically look for a functioning canteen with adequate seating, hygienic food preparation, and proper maintenance records.

## The creche threshold

A **creche** (childcare facility) is required once an establishment - factory, mine, plantation, or in many states, a shop or commercial establishment - employs **50 or more employees**. This threshold has stayed consistent from the Maternity Benefit Act 1961 through to the Code on Social Security, 2020.

Unlike the canteen rule, the creche threshold is not limited to factories in most state interpretations - it applies more broadly once headcount crosses 50, making it relevant to offices, retail chains and service businesses too.

## Restroom and washroom facilities

Separate toilet and washing facilities for women employees are near-universal requirements regardless of headcount under state Factories Rules and Shops and Establishments Acts. What does scale with headcount is the number of facilities required and, in some states, additional rest-room or lunch-room space once the workforce passes certain size bands.

## Threshold summary table

| Facility | Typical trigger | Governing framework |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canteen | 250+ workers (factory) | Factories Act 1948 / OSH Code, 2020 |
| Creche | 50+ employees | Maternity Benefit Act 1961 / Code on Social Security, 2020 |
| Separate women's restroom | No fixed headcount - near-universal | State Factories Rules / Shops & Establishments Act |
| Rest rooms / lunch rooms | Varies by state, often 150+ workers | State Factories Rules |

## Counting your headcount correctly

Most interpretations count everyone actually working at the establishment on a given day, including **contract and temporary workers**, not just those on direct payroll. If your headcount fluctuates near a threshold - say, between 45 and 55 employees - it is safer to plan for the facility rather than assume you are exempt, since inspectors typically look at peak or average headcount over a period rather than a single day.

## What changes under the Labour Codes

The OSH Code, 2020 and the Code on Social Security, 2020 largely carry forward these existing thresholds rather than rewriting them from scratch. The bigger practical change is procedural: as states notify their own Labour Codes rules through 2026, the specific forms, registers and inspection processes tied to these facilities may shift even though the headcount triggers themselves stay broadly the same.
