Street vendors registered under the **Street Vendors Act 2014** are now deemed to be registered under FSSAI as well, removing a duplicate registration step that previously added friction for one of India's largest informal food business segments.

## Key facts at a glance

- Under the **FSSAI Licensing and Registration Amendment Regulations 2026** (effective 11 March 2026), Street Vendors Act registrants are **deemed registered under FSSAI**.
- This applies to vendors holding a valid **certificate of vending** under the Street Vendors Act 2014.
- Deemed registration **removes the need for a separate FSSAI Basic Registration application** for these vendors.
- Vendors are **still subject to food safety inspection and enforcement**, deemed status is not an exemption from compliance.
- The change is part of a broader 2026 shift toward **risk-based inspection**, including third-party audits where directed.
- Vendors scaling beyond typical street vending volumes may eventually need a **higher FSSAI tier**.

## Why this change was made

Street food vendors form a large part of India's informal food economy, and many previously either skipped FSSAI registration or found the process duplicative given they already register under the Street Vendors Act 2014 with their local urban body. The 2026 amendment recognises that overlap and removes the redundant step, linking FSSAI compliance directly to the vendor's existing Street Vendors Act status.

This is a genuine simplification, not a loosening of food safety standards - it changes which form you fill, not what hygiene practices you are expected to follow.

## What street vendors still need to do

### Keep the certificate of vending current

Since deemed FSSAI registration flows from your Street Vendors Act registration, keeping that certificate valid and up to date is now doubly important. If it lapses, your deemed FSSAI status is affected too.

### Maintain basic food safety practices

Deemed registration does not touch food safety obligations. Vendors should still follow basic hygiene practices - clean water, covered food storage, proper waste disposal - since FSSAI inspection and enforcement authority continues to apply.

### Watch for scale triggers

A vendor who grows into a larger, higher-turnover operation (a bigger stall, multiple locations, packaged product sales) may cross into territory where a standard FSSAI registration or licence is more appropriate than relying on deemed status. There is no single bright line here, so it's worth checking in as your business grows.

## Street Vendors Act registration vs. standalone FSSAI registration

| Aspect | Street Vendors Act (deemed FSSAI) | Standalone FSSAI Basic Registration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issuing authority | Urban local body | FSSAI / state food safety department |
| Applies to | Registered street vendors | Any food business up to Rs 1.5 crore turnover |
| Separate FSSAI application | Not required (deemed) | Required |
| Food safety obligations | Still apply | Still apply |
| Suitable for | Typical street vending scale | Small businesses of any format, including higher-volume vendors |

## What this means in practice for city-level enforcement

Municipal and food safety authorities can now treat a valid Street Vendors Act certificate as sufficient proof of FSSAI registration during routine checks, which should reduce harassment of vendors who previously had no easy way to show dual compliance. Vendors should still carry their certificate of vending visibly, since that is now the document doing double duty.
