Dealership workshops run on a mix of direct-hire technicians, helpers, and often contract labour supplied through a workshop staffing contractor - and each of these categories carries its own EPF, ESI and contract-labour obligations that dealers frequently underestimate.

## Key facts at a glance

- **EPF is mandatory at 20 or more employees**, with a wage ceiling of **Rs 15,000/month** and **12 percent employee / 12 percent employer** contribution.
- The **EPF Scheme 2026**, effective **29 June 2026**, aligns EPF with the Code on Social Security 2020, but the ceiling and contribution rates are unchanged.
- **ESI is mandatory at 10 or more employees** (1+ in hazardous units), with a wage ceiling of **Rs 21,000/month** (Rs 25,000 for employees with disability).
- ESI contribution is **0.75 percent employee / 3.25 percent employer**.
- The **principal employer** (the dealership) retains supervisory responsibility for EPF/ESI compliance of contract workers on its premises, even when a contractor is the direct employer.
- Contract labour above a state-set threshold typically requires **registration/licensing** under contract labour regulation for both principal employer and contractor.
- EPF compliance is moving toward **heavier electronic filing**, making paper-only contractor assurances harder to rely on.

## Who Counts Toward the Employee Threshold

A common misconception at dealerships is that only "regular" showroom staff count toward EPF and ESI thresholds. In practice, workshop technicians, helpers, valet staff, and other workers on the payroll all count, whether they work at the showroom, the service bay, or a detailing area. Once total headcount crosses 20, EPF registration generally becomes mandatory; ESI kicks in earlier, at 10 employees.

Where dealerships often go wrong is undercounting by excluding contract or third-party staff from this headcount entirely. Depending on how the arrangement is structured, contract workers deployed at the dealership premises may still need to be accounted for under the principal employer's supervisory obligations, even if their direct EPF/ESI contributions are remitted through the contractor.

## Direct Hire vs Contract Labour: Where Responsibility Sits

| Aspect | Direct-hire technician | Contract labour (via contractor) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate employer for EPF/ESI filing | Dealership | Contractor |
| Principal employer's supervisory duty | N/A (dealer is the employer) | Dealer must verify contractor is compliant |
| Contract labour licensing | Not applicable | May be required for both parties above threshold |
| Liability if contributions are not deposited | Dealer's direct liability | Dealer can face liability if contractor defaults |
| Headcount counted toward dealer's own EPF/ESI threshold | Yes | Depends on structure - confirm with a compliance professional |

## Why Contractor Arrangements Do Not Eliminate Risk

Using a staffing contractor for technicians is a common and legitimate business practice, but it does not automatically shift all statutory risk away from the dealership. Under contract-labour regulation, the principal employer generally retains a duty to ensure the contractor is actually depositing EPF and ESI contributions for the workers supplied - not simply paying an invoice and assuming compliance is handled.

If a contractor defaults on contributions, dealers can find themselves facing liability, reputational exposure, or disruption if the contractor's licence lapses. This makes periodic verification of contractor compliance, not a one-time onboarding check, the safer practice.

## Practical Steps for Dealers

Start with a clean headcount audit across showroom, workshop and any detailing or valet functions, including anyone working regularly on the premises regardless of who issues their paycheck. Confirm whether your dealership has crossed the ESI (10 employees) and EPF (20 employees) thresholds, and if you rely on contract labour, periodically request proof of EPF/ESI deposit from your contractor rather than treating the contract as self-policing.

Given the shift toward electronic filing under the EPF Scheme 2026, ask your contractor whether they file electronically and can produce compliance evidence on demand - this is increasingly the practical test of whether a contractor relationship is actually compliant or just assumed to be.
