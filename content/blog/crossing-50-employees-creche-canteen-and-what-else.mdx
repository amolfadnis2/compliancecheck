Crossing **50 employees** puts a **crèche facility** on your compliance checklist immediately, with canteen and other welfare obligations following as you keep growing.

## Key facts at a glance

- A **crèche facility** becomes mandatory for establishments with **50 or more employees** under the Maternity Benefit Act (as amended).
- The creche headcount counts **all employees**, not just women employees.
- **Canteen** requirements typically apply at a higher threshold - **100 or more workers** for factories under the newer OSH Code, historically **250** under the earlier Factories Act.
- Many employers satisfy the creche requirement through a **shared or outsourced facility** rather than an in-house one.
- The **next major threshold** after 50 is generally 100 employees, where works committee and standing orders obligations begin.
- A **welfare officer** becomes mandatory in factories only at **500 workers**.

## Creche: the obligation that starts right now

The **Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act, 2017** requires every establishment with **50 or more employees** to provide a crèche facility, either on its own premises or within a prescribed distance. Importantly, the 50-employee count is based on total employees, not just women employees or mothers, so this applies broadly once you cross the line.

Employers commonly meet this obligation through a dedicated in-house room, a tie-up with a nearby creche provider, or a shared facility serving multiple establishments in the same building or industrial area. Whichever route you choose, it needs to be a real, accessible facility - not just a policy statement.

## Canteen: coming soon, not yet due

The word "canteen" in the same breath as crèche makes many founders think both kick in at 50. They don't, quite. Canteen obligations for factories are pegged to a higher headcount - **100 or more workers** under the newer Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code (a reduction from the **250-worker** threshold under the earlier Factories Act, 1948, for factories that remain under that regime pending full state notification of the new Codes).

The practical takeaway: at 50 employees, canteen isn't legally due yet, but it's close enough that budgeting and space planning should start now rather than waiting for the trigger to hit.

## What else changes around this size

Fifty employees is also roughly where businesses start feeling the weight of other welfare-linked obligations even if the strict legal thresholds sit slightly higher or lower depending on the law - first aid provisioning scales with headcount, and if you operate a factory, safety committee expectations often become a practical necessity even before they're a strict legal requirement in your state.

## What changes, at a glance

| Obligation | Trigger | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crèche facility | 50+ employees | Establishments generally (Maternity Benefit Act) |
| Canteen | 100+ workers (OSH Code) / 250+ (older Factories Act) | Factories |
| Works committee, standing orders | 100+ workers | Industrial establishments |
| Welfare officer | 500+ workers | Factories |

## What to do this week

Confirm your total headcount against the 50-employee creche trigger and, if you're covered, arrange either an in-house facility or a shared/outsourced tie-up. Separately, start tracking your trajectory toward 100 employees so canteen, works committee and standing orders obligations don't catch you by surprise.
