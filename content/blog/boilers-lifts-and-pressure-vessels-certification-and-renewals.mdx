Boilers, lifts and certain pressure vessels each sit under their own certification regime in India, with different inspecting authorities, renewal cycles and paperwork - and missing any one of them is treated as a safety violation, not just a paperwork lapse.

## Key facts at a glance

- **Boilers** are regulated under the Indian Boilers Act and Regulations, with certification issued by the state **Boiler Inspectorate**.
- Boiler certificates are typically **renewed annually** after a periodic inspection, though the schedule can vary by boiler class.
- **Lifts and escalators** are regulated under state-specific **Lift Acts**, administered by the state Electrical Inspectorate or a dedicated Lifts Department.
- **Pressure vessels** storing or handling gas or liquid under pressure can fall under the **Static and Mobile Pressure Vessels Rules**, administered by the **Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO)**.
- Certification requirements are usually based on the **equipment's type and capacity**, not overall business size, so even small units can be in scope.
- Each category has its **own separate inspecting authority**, so a single premises with a boiler, a lift and a pressure vessel may need to deal with three different regulators.
- Operating any of this equipment on a **lapsed certificate** is generally treated as a safety violation with penalty exposure, independent of whether an accident actually occurs.

## Why these three are grouped together

Boilers, lifts and pressure vessels share a common thread: each is mechanical equipment where a failure can cause serious injury or death, so each is regulated with its own dedicated inspecting authority and mandatory periodic re-certification, rather than a one-time approval. Businesses that operate manufacturing units, commercial buildings with lifts, or process equipment involving pressurised gas or liquid typically need to track all three separately.

## Boilers

Boiler certification falls under the Indian Boilers Act framework, administered at the state level by the Boiler Inspectorate. A boiler cannot legally be operated without a current certificate, and the inspecting authority typically re-examines the boiler periodically - commonly on an annual cycle - checking structural integrity, safety valves and operating pressure limits.

### What triggers boiler regulation

Any pressure vessel used to generate steam for a purpose other than a specifically excluded use is likely to fall under boiler regulation, which is why even a canteen or process boiler in a smaller facility can be in scope, not just large industrial boilers.

## Lifts and escalators

Unlike boilers, lifts are regulated purely at the state level under each state's own Lift Act and Rules, since there is no single central law. This means the specific inspection frequency, licensing of maintenance agencies, and documentation required can differ meaningfully between states. Installation typically requires prior approval, and operation requires a periodic renewal inspection by the state's Electrical Inspectorate or equivalent Lifts Department.

## Pressure vessels

Pressure vessels used to store or process compressed gas or liquid, particularly hazardous or flammable substances, can fall under the Static and Mobile Pressure Vessels Rules administered by PESO. This is distinct from ordinary boiler regulation and applies based on the vessel's contents and pressure rating rather than its use for steam generation. Businesses storing LPG, industrial gases or similar substances under pressure should check whether their specific vessels are in scope.

## Certification snapshot

| Equipment | Governing framework | Typical regulator | Renewal pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boilers | Indian Boilers Act and Regulations | State Boiler Inspectorate | Commonly annual, class-dependent |
| Lifts / escalators | State-specific Lift Acts and Rules | State Electrical Inspectorate / Lifts Department | Periodic, varies by state |
| Pressure vessels | Static and Mobile Pressure Vessels Rules | PESO, with state coordination | Periodic, capacity/content-dependent |

## Practical compliance approach

Because each of these three has a separate authority and separate renewal clock, the most reliable approach is to maintain one internal register listing every regulated boiler, lift and pressure vessel on your premises, along with its certificate number, issuing authority and next renewal date. Treat renewal the same way you would treat any other statutory licence renewal - flagged well ahead of the expiry date, not reacted to after it lapses.

For businesses operating across multiple states, remember that lift regulation in particular is state-specific, so a compliance checklist that works for one location cannot simply be copied to a facility in another state without checking that state's own Lift Act requirements.
