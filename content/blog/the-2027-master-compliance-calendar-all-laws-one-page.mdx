A single, reliable compliance calendar is the difference between routine filings and last-minute penalty notices - here is a one-page view of the recurring and fixed 2027 deadlines that apply to most Indian employers and companies.

## Key facts at a glance

- **PF contributions** are due by the **15th of the following month**; ESI contributions follow the same **15th** deadline.
- **TDS payments** are due by the **7th of the following month** (except March, which has a later deadline).
- Company **AGMs** are generally required within **six months** of financial year end (by 30 September for a March year-end).
- **Form AOC-4** is due within **30 days** of the AGM; **Form MGT-7/MGT-7A** within **60 days** of the AGM.
- Companies requiring a **tax audit** face a **30 September** tax audit report deadline and **31 October** ITR deadline.
- The **DPDP Act's** full substantive obligations become enforceable from **13 May 2027**.
- **GST annual return** (Form GSTR-9) is typically due by **31 December** following the financial year.

## How to use this calendar

Treat the deadlines below as anchors, not a complete list - your actual obligations depend on your entity type, headcount, turnover, sector and state. Use this as the skeleton and layer your sector-specific dates (FSSAI renewal, factory licence renewal, S&E Act renewal) on top of it.

### Monthly recurring deadlines

| Filing | Due date | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PF contribution (ECR) | 15th of following month | Employers with 20+ employees |
| ESI contribution | 15th of following month | Employers with 10+ employees (1+ hazardous) |
| Professional tax (varies by state) | State-specific, often 15th-20th | Employers per state P.Tax rules |
| TDS payment | 7th of following month | All tax deductors |
| GST GSTR-1 (monthly filers) | 11th of following month | Regular GST taxpayers |
| GST GSTR-3B | 20th of following month (varies by category) | Regular GST taxpayers |

### Quarterly deadlines

| Filing | Typical due date | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TDS return (Form 24Q/26Q) | End of month following quarter | All tax deductors |
| GST QRMP scheme return | 22nd/24th of month after quarter (state-dependent) | Small taxpayers under QRMP |
| Advance tax instalments | 15 Jun, 15 Sep, 15 Dec, 15 Mar | Businesses with tax liability above threshold |

### Annual deadlines through 2027

| Filing / event | Typical due date | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AGM | Within 6 months of FY end (by 30 Sep for Mar FY end) | All companies except OPCs |
| Form AOC-4 | Within 30 days of AGM | All companies |
| Form MGT-7 / MGT-7A | Within 60 days of AGM | All companies |
| Tax audit report | 30 September | Entities subject to tax audit |
| Income tax return (audit cases) | 31 October | Companies and audited entities |
| Income tax return (non-audit cases) | 31 July | Individuals, non-audit businesses |
| GST annual return (GSTR-9) | 31 December | Regular GST taxpayers above threshold |
| FLA return (FEMA) | 15 July | Companies with FDI or overseas investment |
| POSH Internal Committee annual report | Per scheme year, typically Dec-Jan | Employers with 10+ employees |

## The 2027-specific dates to circle

Two deadlines are unusual to 2027 and worth flagging separately from the routine calendar above. First, the **DPDP Act's** full substantive obligations - notice requirements, consent mechanics, security safeguards, breach reporting, and data-principal rights - become enforceable from **13 May 2027**, with no stated grace period, so any business handling personal data digitally should have its compliance programme operational well before that date, not starting on it.

Second, the four **Labour Codes** (Wages, Industrial Relations, Social Security, and Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions) came into force nationally on **21 November 2025**, but because labour is a concurrent subject, each state must separately notify its own rules before the Codes are fully operational there. As of mid-2026 most states had not yet fully notified, so through 2027 employers should track their specific state's notification status rather than assume uniform, nationwide applicability on a single date.

## Building your own calendar

A generic calendar like this one is a starting point, not a substitute for one tailored to your business. The dates that actually matter to you depend on:

- **Entity type** - private limited company, LLP, partnership or proprietorship each have different ROC and tax obligations.
- **Headcount** - PF kicks in at 20+ employees, ESI at 10+ (1+ for hazardous units), POSH ICC at 10+.
- **State** - professional tax, Shops & Establishments renewal, and Labour Code rollout timing all vary by state.
- **Sector** - FSSAI, fire safety, factory licensing and sector-specific renewals add their own dates.

The safest practice is to consolidate every applicable deadline into one shared calendar with reminders set at least two weeks in advance, and to name a single owner per filing so nothing falls between departments.
