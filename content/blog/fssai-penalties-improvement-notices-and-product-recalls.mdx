FSSAI enforcement generally moves through stages - inspection, improvement notice, and only then stronger action like suspension, penalty, or recall - so understanding each stage helps a food business respond correctly rather than panic at the first notice.

## Key facts at a glance

- Inspections in 2026 are **risk-based**, not fixed calendar-based, following the FSSAI Licensing and Registration Amendment Regulations 2026 (effective 11 March 2026).
- **Third-party audits** by FSSAI-empanelled agencies can now feed into the same enforcement pipeline as government inspections.
- An **improvement notice** is typically the first formal enforcement step, giving a specific timeline to fix identified issues.
- Continued non-compliance after a notice can lead to **licence suspension or cancellation**.
- **Product recalls** are triggered when a food item poses a genuine safety or serious mislabelling risk.
- FSSAI registrations/licences issued from **1 April 2026 have no fixed expiry**, but remain subject to suspension or cancellation for non-compliance regardless.

## The enforcement pipeline, step by step

### 1. Inspection or audit

This can come from a government food safety officer or, under the 2026 risk-based regime, a third-party audit agency empanelled by FSSAI. The outcome is a report against food safety and hygiene standards.

### 2. Improvement notice

If gaps are found that are not severe enough for immediate suspension, the business receives an improvement notice specifying the issues and a deadline to fix them. This is the stage where most non-compliance gets resolved without escalating further.

### 3. Follow-up verification

Authorities may re-inspect or request evidence that the corrective actions in the notice have been completed. Ignoring the notice or providing inadequate fixes moves the case toward stronger enforcement.

### 4. Suspension, cancellation, or penalty

For unresolved or serious violations, FSSAI or the state authority can suspend or cancel the licence, impose monetary penalties, or in serious cases pursue prosecution under the Food Safety and Standards Act.

### 5. Product recall (where applicable)

Separate from licence-level enforcement, a specific product can be recalled if it is found unsafe or seriously mislabelled, regardless of whether the business's overall licence is otherwise in order.

## What escalates a case faster

Some issues bypass the gradual improvement-notice path and move to faster, harder enforcement:

- Evidence of adulteration or contamination posing immediate health risk.
- Repeat violations after a prior improvement notice was supposedly resolved.
- Operating without any valid FSSAI registration or licence at all.
- Selling a product already flagged for recall.

## Enforcement stages at a glance

| Stage | Typical Trigger | Business Response Needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspection/audit | Routine or risk-based scheduling | Cooperate, provide records |
| Improvement notice | Minor to moderate gaps found | Fix issues within given timeline |
| Follow-up check | Notice deadline reached | Demonstrate corrective action |
| Suspension/cancellation | Unresolved or serious violation | Legal/compliance response required |
| Product recall | Unsafe or seriously mislabelled product | Immediate removal from sale, notify authority |

## Practical steps to reduce enforcement risk

Treat every inspection and audit as an opportunity to close gaps before they become notices. Keep hygiene, pest control, and staff health records current and accessible, since most improvement notices trace back to documentation or practice gaps that were fixable in advance. If you do receive a notice, respond within the given timeline rather than letting it lapse - the businesses that face suspension are typically those that ignored an earlier warning, not first-time offenders.
