Preparing your business for **acquisition** means clearing **compliance skeletons** before a buyer's due diligence team finds them, since unresolved gaps translate directly into lower valuations, escrow holdbacks or a stalled deal.

## Key facts at a glance

- **EPF registration** is mandatory once a company crosses **20 employees**; buyers routinely reconcile registration and contribution history against actual historical headcount.
- **ESI registration** is mandatory once a company crosses **10 employees** (1+ in hazardous units); wage ceiling is **Rs 21,000/month** (Rs 25,000 for employees with disability).
- **POSH Internal Committee** is mandatory at **10 or more employees**; its absence is one of the most frequently flagged findings in acquisition diligence.
- M&A due diligence commonly reviews **3-5 years** of compliance history, longer than typical funding-round diligence.
- In a **share sale**, the buyer generally inherits the target's pre-existing statutory liabilities, including any unpaid contributions or pending disputes.
- Compliance gaps found during diligence commonly result in a **price adjustment**, an **escrow holdback**, or a **specific indemnity clause** rather than an outright deal collapse.

## Why buyers scrutinise compliance so closely

An acquirer is not just buying revenue and customers, it is buying the target company's history, including every unresolved statutory obligation attached to it. In a share sale, this history transfers with the entity through the principle of successor liability, meaning unpaid EPF contributions, unfiled returns, or a missing POSH committee from years earlier become the buyer's problem the moment the deal closes. This is why compliance diligence in M&A is typically more thorough and looks back further than compliance diligence for a funding round.

## The most common skeletons found

### Headcount-based registrations that were never triggered
Companies frequently cross the EPF (20 employees) or ESI (10 employees) thresholds without formally registering, sometimes for years, either through oversight or because headcount fluctuated near the threshold. Buyers reconstruct historical headcount from payroll records and flag any period where the company should have been registered but was not.

### Missing or inactive POSH Internal Committee
This is one of the single most common findings, since many SMEs treat POSH as a documentation exercise done once rather than an ongoing obligation with annual reporting requirements. A missing Internal Committee at 10+ employees is now flagged even by generalist M&A counsel, not just specialist labour lawyers.

### Inconsistent tax filings
Mismatches between GST returns, TDS returns and financial statements, even where they arise from genuine timing differences rather than wrongdoing, slow down diligence and invite deeper scrutiny of everything else.

### Informal employment arrangements
Employees without written appointment letters, or with contract terms that do not match actual practice (title, compensation, notice period), create both a compliance gap and a valuation question about the true cost of formalising the workforce post-acquisition.

### Lapsed sector licences
A lapsed FSSAI licence, an expired trade licence, or an unrenewed state-specific registration can each independently delay or derail a deal, since the buyer cannot legally continue certain operations until the licence is renewed or transferred.

## Skeleton severity guide

| Gap category | Typical buyer response | Remediation difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing POSH Internal Committee | Pre-closing condition or immediate post-closing fix | Low - can be resolved in weeks |
| Unregistered EPF/ESI despite eligible headcount | Escrow holdback or indemnity for back-contributions | Medium - requires back-payment and penalty exposure assessment |
| Informal employment contracts | Price or indemnity adjustment | Medium - requires renegotiating terms with existing staff |
| Lapsed sector licence (e.g. FSSAI) | Closing condition - deal often cannot close until resolved | Medium to high, depending on renewal timeline |
| Long-running unfiled tax returns | Deep additional diligence, possible walk-away | High - may require professional remediation before sale |

## Starting the cleanup early

The realistic runway needed to clear most compliance skeletons is measured in months, not weeks, particularly for headcount-based registrations that require back-filing or for licences with a multi-week renewal process. Starting a compliance review 6-12 months before you plan to begin acquisition conversations gives you time to fix what is fixable and prepare an honest, documented explanation for anything that is not.
