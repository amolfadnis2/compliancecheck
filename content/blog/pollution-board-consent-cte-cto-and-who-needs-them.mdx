Any unit likely to generate air emissions, effluent, or industrial waste in India typically needs **Consent to Establish (CTE)** before construction and **Consent to Operate (CTO)** before starting operations, both issued by the State Pollution Control Board.

## Key facts at a glance

- **CTE (Consent to Establish)** must be obtained **before** you construct or set up your unit.
- **CTO (Consent to Operate)** must be obtained **before** you start production, and renewed periodically thereafter.
- Both consents are issued under the **Water Act, 1974** and **Air Act, 1981** by the **State Pollution Control Board (SPCB)**.
- Industries are classified into **red, orange, green and white categories** based on pollution potential, which determines process complexity.
- **White category** units are often exempt from full consent or covered by simplified registration, but this varies by state.
- CTO validity commonly ranges from **1 to 5 years** depending on category and state, after which renewal is mandatory.

## What CTE actually covers

Consent to Establish is the approval you need before breaking ground on a new unit, or before materially expanding or modifying an existing one. The SPCB reviews your proposed activity, location, expected emissions or effluent, and pollution control measures before granting CTE. Skipping this step and starting construction anyway is a common compliance gap that surfaces later during CTO applications or inspections.

## What CTO actually covers

Consent to Operate is the go-live approval - it confirms that what you actually built matches what was approved under CTE, and that your pollution control equipment (effluent treatment plant, air scrubbers, etc.) is functioning as intended. CTO is not a one-time approval; it must be **renewed periodically**, and operating past expiry without renewal is treated the same as operating without consent at all.

## Who actually needs CTE/CTO

Broadly, any unit involved in manufacturing, processing, or an activity capable of generating air emissions, water discharge, or hazardous waste needs some form of pollution board consent. This includes obvious cases like factories and chemical units, but also extends to activities such as printing, dyeing, food processing, workshops, and even some service establishments depending on their state's classification rules.

### Category-based approach

State Pollution Control Boards classify industries using a **colour-coded category system** that determines how much scrutiny and paperwork applies.

| Category | Typical pollution potential | Consent complexity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | High | Full CTE + CTO, frequent renewal, detailed monitoring |
| Orange | Moderate | CTE + CTO required, moderate documentation |
| Green | Low | CTE + CTO required but simplified process |
| White | Negligible | Often exempt or simplified registration only |

The category your activity falls into is state-specific and periodically revised, so the same business type can sit in different categories across states.

## Applying for consent

Most states now run CTE/CTO applications through an online single-window or SPCB portal, requiring project details, site plans, expected emissions/effluent data, and pollution control equipment specifications. Processing timelines vary by state and category, with lower-risk categories generally moving faster than red category applications.

## Renewal and ongoing compliance

Once CTO is granted, the obligation does not end - most SPCBs require periodic **self-monitoring reports**, effluent/emission testing, and timely renewal applications well before expiry. Many states also expect renewal applications to be filed a fixed number of days before the current CTO lapses, so tracking your renewal calendar is as important as getting the initial consent.

## What happens without valid consent

Operating without CTE, without CTO, or on an expired CTO exposes a business to **closure orders, monetary penalties, and denial of utility connections** (many states link electricity and water connections to valid pollution consent). For SMEs, this can mean a sudden production stoppage with limited notice, making proactive renewal tracking a genuinely business-critical task rather than a paperwork afterthought.
