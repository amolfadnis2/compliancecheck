The **Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013** — the POSH Act — requires most Indian workplaces to set up a complaints mechanism and follow defined procedures. This guide focuses on the **Internal Committee (ICC)**, the cornerstone of compliance.

## Who must form an Internal Committee?

Every workplace with **10 or more employees** must constitute an Internal Committee (often called the ICC or IC) at each office or branch where 10 or more are employed. Workplaces below that threshold are covered by a Local Committee at the district level.

## How the Internal Committee must be composed

The committee must include:

- **A Presiding Officer** — a woman employed at a senior level.
- **At least two members** from among employees, preferably committed to the cause of women or with relevant experience.
- **One external member** from an NGO or association working on women's issues, or someone familiar with sexual-harassment issues.

**At least half** of the members must be women.

## Core compliance obligations

1. **Constitute the ICC** and notify members at each qualifying location.
2. **Display the penal consequences** of sexual harassment and ICC details prominently at the workplace.
3. **Adopt a POSH policy** defining harassment, the complaint process, and timelines.
4. **Conduct awareness programmes** for employees and **orientation** for ICC members.
5. **Handle complaints** within the statutory timelines — inquiry generally completed within 90 days.
6. **File an annual report** with the District Officer.
7. **Include POSH compliance** in your directors' report where applicable.

## Timelines that matter

- A complaint can be filed within **3 months** of an incident (extendable in certain cases).
- The inquiry should be **completed within 90 days**.
- The employer must act on recommendations within **60 days** of receiving the report.

## Why it matters

Beyond the legal duty, non-compliance can lead to fines, cancellation of business licences on repeat offences, and significant reputational risk. A properly constituted, trained ICC is the single most important step.
