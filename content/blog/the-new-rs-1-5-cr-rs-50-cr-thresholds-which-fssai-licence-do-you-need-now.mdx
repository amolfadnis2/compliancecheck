FSSAI now uses **Rs 1.5 crore** annual turnover as the cutoff for Registration and **Rs 50 crore** as the cutoff for a State Licence, both raised under the 2026 amendment regulations. Getting your category right avoids either under-licensing your business or paying for a licence tier you do not need.

## Key facts at a glance

- The **FSSAI Registration** threshold is now annual turnover **up to Rs 1.5 crore**.
- The **State Licence** threshold is now annual turnover **up to Rs 50 crore**.
- Turnover **above Rs 50 crore**, or specific business activities, generally requires a **Central Licence**.
- These revised thresholds came from the **FSSAI Licensing and Registration Amendment Regulations 2026**, effective **11 March 2026**.
- **Street vendors** registered under the Street Vendors Act, 2014 are **deemed registered** under FSSAI.
- Turnover is the primary factor, but **business activity type** (import, export, multi-state operation) can also determine the required category.
- Operating without the correct licence category is a compliance gap even if you hold *some* form of FSSAI approval.

## The Three FSSAI Categories

FSSAI licensing works on a tiered system based mainly on annual turnover, with three broad categories:

1. **Registration** - for the smallest food businesses, including many small vendors, home-based businesses and petty food operators.
2. **State Licence** - for mid-sized food businesses operating within a single state.
3. **Central Licence** - for large businesses, those operating across multiple states, importers/exporters, and certain categories regardless of turnover.

## Turnover Thresholds at a Glance

| Category | Approximate annual turnover | Typical business examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration | Up to Rs 1.5 crore | Small eateries, home kitchens, petty food stalls, small manufacturers |
| State Licence | Above Rs 1.5 crore, up to Rs 50 crore | Mid-sized restaurants, regional manufacturers, catering businesses |
| Central Licence | Above Rs 50 crore, or specific activities | Large manufacturers, importers/exporters, multi-state chains, certain high-risk categories |

Note that these are turnover-based bands under the current regulations; the exact treatment of your business can also depend on the nature of your operations, so confirm your specific category with FSSAI or a licensing professional if you are close to a threshold.

## Why the Thresholds Were Raised

Raising the Registration threshold to Rs 1.5 crore and the State Licence threshold to Rs 50 crore effectively moves a large number of small and mid-sized food businesses into a lighter compliance category than before. For a business that was previously required to hold a State Licence but now falls under Registration by turnover, this can mean simpler paperwork and different renewal obligations, especially combined with the move to **perpetual validity** for new licences and registrations issued from 1 April 2026.

## What to Do If Your Category Has Changed

If your turnover puts you in a different category than the licence you currently hold, do not assume the old licence automatically covers you correctly going forward. Steps to take:

- **Recalculate your current annual turnover** against the new thresholds.
- **Compare it to your existing licence category** (Registration, State, or Central).
- If there is a mismatch, **check with FSSAI or a licensing consultant** on the correct process to move categories.
- Keep the change **documented** in case it comes up during a risk-based inspection.

## Common Mistakes Businesses Make

- Assuming turnover thresholds are unchanged from before 2026 and staying on an outdated category.
- Confusing "turnover" with "profit" - FSSAI thresholds are based on gross annual turnover, not net income.
- Ignoring activity-based triggers (import/export, multi-state operations) that can require a Central Licence regardless of turnover.
- Not updating the licence category after crossing a threshold mid-year.
