A home-based food business in India needs, at minimum, an **FSSAI Basic Registration** before selling any food to the public - that single step is non-negotiable regardless of how small the operation is.

## Key facts at a glance

- **FSSAI Basic Registration** is required for any food business selling to the public, including home-based ones, with no lower turnover exemption.
- The **registration turnover threshold** is up to **Rs 1.5 crore** annually under the 2026 amendment regulations.
- FSSAI registrations issued from **1 April 2026** have **no fixed expiry date**.
- **GST registration** becomes mandatory once turnover crosses the statutory threshold, and is often useful earlier for platform selling.
- **Shop and establishment registration** requirements for home-based operations vary significantly by state.
- Basic hygiene and food safety standards apply to home kitchens **just as they do to commercial ones**, even without a dedicated commercial kitchen requirement.

## Why FSSAI registration is the starting point, not optional

Under the Food Safety and Standards Act, anyone manufacturing, selling, or distributing food - including from a home kitchen - is a "food business operator" and needs either FSSAI registration or a licence, depending on turnover. There is no informal or "too small to count" exemption once you are selling regularly, even if it's only through WhatsApp orders or a local delivery group.

Operating without any FSSAI registration exposes you to penalties and the risk of a stop-sale order, and it also blocks you from listing on any delivery platform, since Swiggy and Zomato both require an FSSAI number at onboarding.

## What the Basic Registration process actually involves

Basic Registration is the lightest tier of FSSAI approval, meant for small operators including home kitchens, small manufacturers, and petty food businesses. It typically requires:

- A simple application with business and owner identity details.
- A declaration of the food category being sold (bakery, tiffin service, pickles, and so on).
- Basic hygiene self-compliance, without the more extensive facility inspection required for a full State Licence.

Because it is the lightest tier, most home-based operators fall comfortably under it unless they scale into a much larger commercial operation.

## Other approvals to check for your state

Beyond FSSAI, a home-based food business may also need:

- **Shop and establishment registration**, depending on whether the state treats a home-based commercial food operation as an establishment requiring registration.
- **GST registration**, mandatory above the statutory turnover threshold, and often practically necessary earlier if you plan to sell through delivery platforms or to GST-registered buyers.
- **Local municipal trade licence or health permit**, which some cities require in addition to FSSAI, particularly for anything beyond very small-scale home selling.

## Minimum setup by business stage

| Stage | FSSAI | GST | Other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Just starting, low volume | Basic Registration | Usually not required yet | Check local municipal rules |
| Selling on delivery platforms | Basic Registration or higher | Often requested regardless of threshold | Shop & establishment (varies) |
| Turnover approaching Rs 1.5 crore | Move to State Licence | Mandatory above GST threshold | Reassess local permits |
| Scaling to a dedicated kitchen/staff | State Licence likely | Mandatory | Shop & establishment, labour compliance |

## Keeping the setup compliant as you grow

The minimum legal setup is not a one-time checkbox - as turnover rises past the registration threshold, you must upgrade from Basic Registration to a State Licence. Track your rolling annual turnover so you are not caught operating under the wrong FSSAI tier, since this is one of the more common compliance gaps among growing home-based food businesses.
