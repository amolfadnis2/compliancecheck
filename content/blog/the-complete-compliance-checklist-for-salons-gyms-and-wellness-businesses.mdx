Salons, gyms and wellness businesses look simple to run but sit under more overlapping licensing than most founders expect, from municipal trade licences to fire safety and labour law. Here is what actually applies.

## Key facts at a glance

- Most states require **Shops and Establishments Act registration** for salons and gyms within a short window of opening, regardless of size.
- **GST registration** is mandatory once annual turnover crosses roughly **Rs 20 lakh** for service businesses (lower thresholds apply in some special category states).
- A **POSH Internal Committee** is mandatory once a workplace has **10 or more employees**.
- **ESI** becomes mandatory at **10+ employees**, with a wage ceiling of **Rs 21,000/month**.
- **EPF** becomes mandatory at **20+ employees**, with a wage ceiling of **Rs 15,000/month**.
- Most local municipal bodies require a **trade licence**, often with hygiene or health conditions specific to salons and spas.
- Commercial premises above a certain occupant load typically need a **fire safety NOC** from the state fire department.

## Shops and Establishments registration comes first

Before anything else, a salon, gym or spa is a commercial establishment under state law, and almost every state requires registration under its Shops and Establishments Act soon after opening. This registration governs working hours, weekly holidays, and employment conditions for staff, and is usually the document local authorities and banks ask for first when verifying that a business is legitimate. Renewal cycles and fees vary by state, so tracking the renewal date matters as much as the initial registration.

## Trade licence and hygiene conditions from the municipal body

Separate from state-level registration, the local municipal corporation or panchayat typically issues a trade licence specific to the business category. For salons and spas, this often comes with hygiene, ventilation and waste-disposal conditions given the personal-care nature of the service; for gyms, conditions may focus on equipment safety and occupant load. Operating without a valid trade licence is a common reason for local-body inspection notices, even when every other compliance is in order.

## Fire safety NOC

Once a premises crosses the occupant-load or built-up-area threshold set by the state fire department, commonly the case for gyms with equipment floors and larger salon chains, a fire safety no-objection certificate becomes mandatory both at fit-out stage and for ongoing renewal. Smaller single-room outlets should still confirm the local threshold rather than assume they are exempt, since thresholds differ by state and by building type.

## GST registration and invoicing

Salons and gyms cross into mandatory GST registration once annual turnover exceeds the applicable service-sector threshold, commonly around **Rs 20 lakh** (with lower thresholds in certain special category states). Membership fees, packages and add-on retail sales (skincare products, supplements) may attract different GST treatment, so businesses selling both services and products should confirm the correct classification and rate for each revenue line.

## Employer compliance for salon and gym staff

Salons and gyms are labour-intensive by nature, which brings standard employer obligations into play earlier than many owners expect.

| Requirement | Trigger |
| --- | --- |
| Shops & Establishments registration | Any commercial premises, state-specific |
| ESI | 10+ employees, Rs 21,000 wage ceiling |
| EPF | 20+ employees, Rs 15,000 wage ceiling |
| POSH Internal Committee | 10+ employees |
| Trade licence | Local municipal body, category-specific |
| Fire NOC | Occupant load/area above state threshold |

## POSH is not optional once you cross 10 employees

Salons and gyms often have a mixed-gender workforce working in close physical proximity to clients and colleagues, which makes POSH compliance particularly relevant, not just a formality. Once headcount reaches **10 employees**, an Internal Committee with a senior woman employee as presiding officer and an external member is legally required, along with a visible policy and annual training. Below that threshold, complaints route to the district Local Committee instead, but displaying a basic anti-harassment policy is still good practice.

## Consumer protection on membership and package sales

Gyms and wellness centres commonly sell prepaid memberships and packages, which brings consumer protection law into play around refund terms, auto-renewal disclosures and cancellation rights. Unclear or unfair contract terms can be challenged before consumer forums, and the Central Consumer Protection Authority has increasingly scrutinised misleading fitness and wellness advertising claims.
