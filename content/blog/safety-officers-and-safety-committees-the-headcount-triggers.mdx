Larger factories and hazardous-process units in India are generally required to appoint a dedicated **safety officer** and form a **safety committee**, but the specific worker-count that triggers each obligation depends on your state's rules, and is now being carried forward - and potentially revised - under the **OSH Code**.

## Key facts at a glance

- Under the Factories Act framework, states have commonly set a **safety officer threshold around 500 workers**, though the exact number varies by state.
- **Hazardous processes** can trigger safety officer and safety committee requirements at a **lower headcount** than standard manufacturing, because the risk profile is higher.
- A **safety committee** is a joint worker-management body that reviews safety performance, incidents and near-misses on a periodic basis.
- Safety officer appointments typically need to be **reported to or approved by** the state factories or labour department, not just made internally.
- The **OSH Code, 2020** carries forward the general concept of safety officers and committees for larger or higher-risk establishments, in force nationally since **21 November 2025**.
- Because labour is a **concurrent subject**, the exact headcount thresholds under the OSH Code depend on each **state's notified rules**, and most states had not fully notified theirs as of mid-2026.
- Safety officer qualifications commonly include a **recognised diploma or degree in industrial safety** plus relevant experience.

## Why headcount-based triggers exist

The logic behind headcount triggers is straightforward: as a workplace grows, the number of people exposed to any given hazard grows with it, and informal, ad hoc safety oversight becomes harder to sustain. Requiring a dedicated safety officer and a structured safety committee above a certain size formalises safety oversight before an informal approach breaks down.

Hazardous processes are treated differently because the risk itself, not just headcount, is what drives the requirement - a smaller unit running a genuinely hazardous process can be required to meet the same safety officer and committee obligations as a much larger standard manufacturing unit.

## Safety officer: role and appointment

A safety officer is typically expected to be a qualified, often full-time role focused on identifying hazards, investigating incidents, training workers on safe practices, and liaising with the factory inspectorate. Because the role carries real regulatory weight, most states require the appointment itself to be reported to, or approved by, the state factories or labour department, and require the individual to hold a recognised safety qualification.

### What a safety officer typically does

Day-to-day responsibilities usually include conducting safety inspections, maintaining accident and near-miss records, running safety training and drills, and advising management on corrective action after incidents. The role is distinct from general HR or facilities management.

## Safety committee: structure and purpose

A safety committee brings together worker representatives and management to review safety data periodically - typically incidents, near-misses, inspection findings and corrective action status. Unlike the safety officer role, which is an individual appointment, the committee is a joint body meant to give workers a direct voice in safety decisions, not just a management-led function.

## Typical trigger comparison

| Trigger | Safety officer | Safety committee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard manufacturing headcount | Commonly required above roughly 500 workers (state-dependent) | Often required above a lower or similar threshold, state-dependent |
| Hazardous process units | Can be required at lower headcount | Can be required at lower headcount |
| Nature of role | Dedicated, often full-time individual appointment | Joint worker-management body, meets periodically |
| Reporting | Usually reported to / approved by state authority | Usually a formal, minuted body with worker representation |
| Basis under OSH Code | Concept retained, exact number depends on state rules | Concept retained, exact number depends on state rules |

## What is changing, and what is not, under the OSH Code

The OSH Code does not eliminate the idea of headcount and hazard-based safety triggers - it retains the broad structure while aiming for more consistent definitions across what were previously separate state rules under the Factories Act. What is genuinely uncertain right now is the exact number at which each obligation kicks in for your specific state, because that depends on rules your state may not have finalised yet.

The practical implication is the same as with other OSH Code transition questions: keep meeting whatever safety officer and safety committee obligations currently apply to you under your state's existing rules, and update your compliance posture as your state formally notifies its OSH Code rules, rather than assuming a threshold has changed before it actually has.
