FSSAI inspections are no longer tied to a fixed calendar - businesses are now assessed and visited based on **risk factors**, and third-party audits can be directed as part of the process. Preparation now means keeping records audit-ready year-round, not just before a scheduled visit.

## Key facts at a glance

- FSSAI inspections have moved from **fixed calendar-based** scheduling to a **risk-based** model.
- **Third-party audits** can be directed by FSSAI as part of this framework.
- The shift accompanied the **FSSAI Licensing and Registration Amendment Regulations 2026**, effective **11 March 2026**.
- Higher-risk businesses (based on category, history, complaints) are inspected **more frequently**.
- Inspection readiness depends on **documentation**, not just physical premises hygiene.
- **FoSTaC**-trained staff records are part of what inspectors typically check.
- A failed inspection can lead to outcomes ranging from a **corrective notice to licence suspension or cancellation**.

## What "Risk-Based" Actually Means

Under the earlier system, FSSAI inspections generally followed a set schedule regardless of a business's actual compliance record. The risk-based model instead factors in things like:

- The **type of food business** (manufacturing, catering, retail, etc.) and inherent risk level.
- **Past inspection history** and any prior violations.
- **Consumer complaints** received against the business.
- **Scale of operations** and licence category (Registration, State, Central).

A business with a clean track record and low inherent risk may see fewer inspections; one with prior violations, complaints, or a higher-risk category is likely to be inspected more often.

## Types of Inspections You Might Face

| Inspection type | Who conducts it | When it typically happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine risk-based inspection | FSSAI food safety officer | Based on risk profile, not a fixed date |
| Complaint-triggered inspection | FSSAI food safety officer | After a consumer or third-party complaint |
| Third-party audit | FSSAI-empanelled third-party auditor | When directed by FSSAI, often for specific categories or after past issues |
| Follow-up inspection | FSSAI food safety officer | After a corrective action notice, to verify compliance |

## What Inspectors Typically Check

- Validity and correct category of your **FSSAI licence or registration**, and whether it is displayed as required.
- Hygiene and sanitation of premises, storage and food handling areas.
- **Food Safety Management System (FSMS)** records and self-compliance documentation.
- **FoSTaC** training certificates for the required food safety supervisor and staff.
- Sourcing and traceability records for raw materials and ingredients.
- Labelling accuracy on packaged products, where applicable.
- Pest control and waste management logs.

## How to Stay Inspection-Ready Year-Round

Since inspections can happen without a fixed schedule, the practical approach is to treat every day as if an inspector could show up:

1. **Keep your licence current and displayed** wherever required, including any category updates after a turnover threshold change.
2. **Maintain a running FSMS log** rather than reconstructing records right before a visit.
3. **Track FoSTaC certification expiry** for your designated food safety supervisor and relevant staff.
4. **Document supplier and sourcing information** so traceability questions can be answered on the spot.
5. **Address any prior notice or complaint promptly** and keep evidence of the corrective action taken.

## If You Receive a Notice or Fail an Inspection

Outcomes vary by severity - a minor gap may result in a corrective action notice with a timeline to fix it, while serious or repeated violations can lead to suspension or cancellation of your licence. Responding quickly and documenting the fix is generally the most effective way to limit escalation.
