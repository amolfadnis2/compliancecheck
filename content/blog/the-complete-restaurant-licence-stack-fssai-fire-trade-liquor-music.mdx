Running a restaurant legally in India takes more than one licence. **FSSAI** covers food safety, but fire safety, trade licensing, GST, liquor and music approvals each come from different authorities with their own rules.

## Key facts at a glance

- **FSSAI licence** category depends on turnover: Registration up to **Rs 1.5 crore**, State Licence up to **Rs 50 crore**, Central Licence above that.
- A **fire safety NOC** is generally required for commercial food premises, with exact rules set by the **state fire department**.
- A **trade licence** from the local municipal body is typically needed to legally operate the premises.
- **GST registration** is required once annual turnover crosses the applicable threshold.
- A **liquor licence** from the state excise department is needed to serve any alcoholic beverage, including beer and wine.
- A **public performance licence** is generally needed to play recorded or live music commercially.
- Requirements and issuing authorities **vary significantly by state and city** - always confirm locally.

## Why One Licence Is Never Enough

Each regulatory area - food safety, fire risk, local commerce, taxation, alcohol, and copyright - is governed by a different authority with its own application process, inspection regime and renewal cycle. A restaurant that only holds an FSSAI licence is compliant on food safety but may still be operating illegally on fire safety, trade permission, or copyright grounds.

## The Core Licence Stack

| Licence/approval | Issuing authority | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FSSAI Registration/Licence | Food Safety and Standards Authority of India | Food safety and hygiene standards |
| Fire safety NOC | State fire department | Fire prevention and evacuation readiness of the premises |
| Trade licence | Local municipal corporation/municipality | Permission to run a commercial establishment at that location |
| GST registration | Central/state GST authority | Tax registration once turnover crosses the threshold |
| Liquor licence | State excise department | Sale and service of alcoholic beverages |
| Public performance licence | Copyright licensing organisation | Commercial playing of recorded or live music |
| Shops & Establishments registration | State labour department | Registration of the commercial establishment and its working conditions |
| Signage/hoarding permission | Local municipal body | Outdoor signage and branding display |

## FSSAI: The Food Safety Layer

Every restaurant needs FSSAI approval, and the category depends on annual turnover under the current thresholds - Registration up to Rs 1.5 crore, State Licence up to Rs 50 crore, and Central Licence above that or for certain business activities. Licences and registrations issued from 1 April 2026 no longer carry a fixed expiry date, though ongoing compliance obligations continue.

## Fire Safety: Often the Most Overlooked Approval

Fire NOC requirements depend on the seating capacity, floor area and construction type of the premises, and rules differ by state. Restaurants sometimes treat this as optional because it is not checked as frequently as FSSAI, but it is a common gap that surfaces during municipal audits or after an incident, and non-compliance can shut down operations quickly.

## Trade Licence and Shops & Establishments

The trade licence, issued by your local municipal body, is the basic permission to run a commercial establishment at a given address. Separately, most states also require registration under the applicable **Shops and Establishments Act**, which governs working hours, holidays and employment conditions for your staff - a labour compliance requirement distinct from the trade licence.

## Liquor and Music: Activity-Specific Add-Ons

If your restaurant serves alcohol, a **liquor licence** from the state excise department is mandatory, and the rules on hours, categories and fees vary widely by state. If you play music - whether background tracks or live performances - a **public performance licence** is generally required from a copyright licensing organisation, regardless of whether the venue also serves alcohol.

## Building Your Compliance Checklist

A practical approach for a new restaurant:

1. Confirm your **FSSAI category** based on projected turnover.
2. Apply for the **fire NOC** early, since it can affect construction and layout decisions.
3. Register for the **trade licence** and **Shops and Establishments** with your local authorities.
4. Register for **GST** once turnover approaches the threshold.
5. Apply for a **liquor licence** if serving alcohol, well before the planned opening date given typical processing times.
6. Secure a **public performance licence** if music will be played.
