Vehicle scrapping in India is not an informal scrapyard process anymore - it runs through Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facilities (RVSFs) that must be formally authorised before they can legally dismantle a vehicle.

## Key facts at a glance

- An **RVSF** is a **Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility**, the only category of facility authorised to legally scrap end-of-life vehicles.
- RVSF registration is required only for facilities that **carry out scrapping**, not for dealers who merely refer customers to one.
- Owners scrapping a vehicle through an RVSF typically receive a **Certificate of Deposit** confirming deregistration.
- The policy operates under rules notified by the **Ministry of Road Transport and Highways**.
- Vehicle **registration is cancelled** as part of the RVSF scrapping process.
- Facilities must meet prescribed **environmental and safety standards** to obtain and retain RVSF registration.

## Why RVSFs exist

India's Vehicle Scrappage Policy was introduced to phase out old, polluting, and unsafe vehicles through a structured, traceable process, rather than informal scrapyards that handled disposal without proper deregistration or environmental controls. RVSFs are the formal mechanism that makes this traceable - a vehicle scrapped through an RVSF is properly deregistered, and its scrapping is documented in a way that can support downstream benefits like rebates on a new vehicle purchase, where a state or manufacturer scheme offers one.

## What RVSF registration involves

A facility seeking RVSF registration needs to meet standards covering safe dismantling processes, environmentally sound handling of fluids, batteries and hazardous materials, and proper recordkeeping of vehicles received and processed. Registration is handled through the relevant state transport authority, in line with rules notified by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.

## Dealer involvement: registered facility versus referral

| Role | RVSF registration needed? |
| --- | --- |
| Facility that physically dismantles end-of-life vehicles | Yes |
| Dealer that refers customers to a registered RVSF for scrapping | No, but should verify the RVSF's registration status |
| Dealer operating an in-house scrapping unit | Yes, for that unit specifically |

Most dealerships interact with the scrappage ecosystem as a referral point rather than as an operator, helping customers trade in an old vehicle and pointing them toward a registered facility. Even in that role, it is worth confirming the RVSF a dealer partners with actually holds valid, current registration, since a customer's ability to claim any related benefit depends on the scrapping being done through a properly registered facility.

## The Certificate of Deposit

Once a vehicle is scrapped through an RVSF, the owner generally receives a Certificate of Deposit, which serves as documentary proof that the vehicle has been deregistered and dismantled. This certificate is often the basis for any incentive tied to scrapping an old vehicle, such as a rebate offered on the purchase of a replacement vehicle under applicable state or manufacturer schemes. Dealers assisting customers through a trade-in-and-scrap process should keep this certificate in the sale file.

## Registration cancellation is not automatic elsewhere

A vehicle owner cannot simply abandon or informally dispose of a vehicle and expect its registration to lapse. Formal cancellation happens through the RVSF process itself, and until that is complete the vehicle legally remains registered to the owner, which can create liability exposure for unpaid dues, fines, or misuse if it is not properly closed out.

## What dealers should verify before referring customers

Before recommending a scrapping facility to a customer, a dealer should confirm the facility currently holds valid RVSF registration, understand what documentation the customer will receive, and be clear on whether any rebate or incentive tied to scrapping applies in that specific state or for that specific manufacturer scheme, since these vary and are not universal.
