A **notice period** is the time an employee or employer must give before ending employment, and both **notice pay** and **garden leave** are variations on how that obligation gets satisfied in practice. The rules are mostly set by contract, not a single national statute, so what is enforceable depends heavily on what was actually written down.

## Key facts at a glance

- There is **no single national minimum notice period** for all private-sector employees; it is governed by the applicable state Shops and Establishments Act and the employment contract.
- **Notice pay in lieu of notice** lets either party substitute payment equal to notice-period salary instead of the employee working through it.
- **Garden leave** keeps an employee on payroll and bound by their contract while asking them not to attend work, often to protect sensitive information.
- Contractual notice periods **longer than the statutory minimum** are generally enforceable if not unreasonable.
- Notice period terms directly affect the timing and amount of an employee's **full and final settlement**.
- Senior or specialised roles often carry **longer contractual notice periods**, sometimes 60 to 90 days, compared to standard employee terms.
- Whether an employee can unilaterally buy out notice with pay depends on the specific contract language, not a universal right.

## Where notice period rules actually come from

Unlike EPF or ESI, which have clear national wage ceilings, notice periods are primarily a creature of contract layered on top of state-level Shops and Establishments Act minimums. This means two employees in different states, or under different contracts at the same company, can legitimately have different notice obligations. HR should treat the employment contract, not general assumption, as the primary source of truth for any specific employee's notice terms.

## Notice pay in lieu of notice

When either the employer or employee wants to end the relationship without waiting out the full notice period, notice pay lets them substitute money for time. The amount is typically the salary the employee would have earned during the unserved portion of the notice period. This can go either direction: an employer terminating without notice generally owes pay in lieu, and many contracts allow an employee to do the same if they want to leave immediately.

### What "salary" means for this calculation

Contracts vary on whether notice pay is calculated on basic pay alone or on full gross salary including allowances. This should be explicit in the employment agreement; where it is silent, disputes commonly arise over which components count.

## Garden leave: paid, but not free to work elsewhere

Garden leave differs from a simple notice period in one important way: the employee remains employed, paid and bound by their contractual obligations (including confidentiality and often non-compete-adjacent restrictions) while explicitly not working. It is commonly used for roles with access to sensitive commercial information, particularly when the employee is departing for a competitor.

Indian courts generally treat garden leave clauses as enforceable if they are clearly written into the contract and the duration is reasonable relative to the role and industry. An excessively long garden leave period, especially one that functions as a disguised non-compete, is more likely to face scrutiny.

| Mechanism | Employee working? | Employee paid? | Typical use case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard notice period | Yes | Yes | Default offboarding |
| Notice pay in lieu | No | Yes, as a lump sum | Immediate exit by either party |
| Garden leave | No | Yes, ongoing | Protecting sensitive information before departure |
| Unpaid absence during notice | No | No | Only if contract or law specifically allows |

## What HR should get right in the contract itself

Since so much of this area is contract-driven rather than statute-driven, the actual enforceability of any notice, notice-pay or garden-leave clause depends on how clearly it was drafted. Ambiguous language on which salary components count, whether either party can unilaterally buy out notice, and how garden leave interacts with post-employment restrictions is the most common source of later disputes.
