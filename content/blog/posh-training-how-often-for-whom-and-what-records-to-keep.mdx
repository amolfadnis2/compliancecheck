The POSH Act requires employers to run awareness training **at regular intervals**, but does not fix an exact frequency in the statute - which means the real compliance question is less about hitting a specific date and more about whether your training is happening consistently and is properly documented.

## Key facts at a glance

- The POSH Act mandates workshops and **awareness programmes at regular intervals** for employees.
- ICC members require **separate orientation training** distinct from general employee awareness sessions.
- Common defensible practice is **at least annual** refresher training for all staff, plus onboarding training for new joiners.
- **Attendance records** - sign-in sheets, digital logs, or completion certificates - are what get checked during audits or disputes.
- E-learning and online modules are **legally acceptable** as long as content and completion are properly tracked.
- ICC constitution and its associated training obligations apply to any workplace with **10 or more employees**.
- Board Reports increasingly expect disclosure of the number of awareness programmes conducted during the year.

## What the law actually requires

Section 19 of the POSH Act places an affirmative duty on employers to organise workshops and awareness programmes at regular intervals for sensitising employees about the provisions of the Act, and to organise orientation programmes for ICC members. The statute deliberately leaves "regular intervals" undefined rather than fixing a rigid number, which gives employers some flexibility but also means the burden falls on them to establish a cadence that is genuinely regular and defensible, not a one-off session run years ago and never repeated.

## Who needs which kind of training

Not every training session should look the same, because different audiences need different depth.

### General employees

All employees need baseline awareness: what constitutes sexual harassment, the organisation's policy, how to file a complaint, and the protections against retaliation. This is the training that should reach everyone, ideally repeated annually and included in onboarding for new joiners.

### Managers and team leads

Managers often receive complaints informally before any formal filing happens, so a module covering their specific responsibilities - what to do if an employee raises a concern to them directly, how to avoid mishandling confidentiality, and their role in preventing retaliation - adds real practical value beyond the general session.

### ICC members

ICC members need dedicated orientation covering inquiry procedure, evidence handling, principles of natural justice, confidentiality obligations, and how to draft findings and recommendations. This is meaningfully different in depth from general awareness training and should be treated as a distinct training track.

| Audience | Training focus | Recommended cadence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All employees | Policy awareness, what counts as harassment, how to complain | At least annually, plus onboarding |
| Managers / team leads | Handling informal disclosures, anti-retaliation duties | At least annually |
| ICC members | Inquiry procedure, evidence, confidentiality, drafting findings | On appointment, plus periodic refreshers |

## Records that actually matter

When a POSH audit, licensing check, or inquiry challenge arises, what gets examined is documentary evidence that training actually happened - not just a policy stating that it should. Keep attendance sheets or digital sign-in logs, dated session records, the content or slide deck used for each session, and completion logs for e-learning modules. For new joiners, tie training completion to the onboarding checklist so it is not left to chance.

## Practical cadence for most employers

Absent a fixed statutory number, running one comprehensive refresher for all employees annually, a distinct onboarding module for every new joiner within their first few weeks, and a dedicated orientation session whenever ICC membership changes, is a reasonable and defensible baseline that most compliance-conscious employers converge on.
