Liquor licensing in India is entirely a **state subject**, which means a restaurant or bar operator cannot rely on a single national process - each state's excise department sets its own categories, fees, and rules, and they can differ dramatically even between neighbouring states.

## Key facts at a glance

- Liquor licensing is handled by **state excise departments**, not any central authority.
- A liquor licence is **separate from and in addition to** your FSSAI food licence.
- Some states have **full or partial prohibition**; most others observe **dry days** with restricted sale.
- Licences are typically **issued per premises** and generally need **annual renewal**.
- Processing timelines **vary widely by state**, from a few weeks to several months.
- Operating without a valid licence risks **penalties, stock seizure, and criminal liability** under state excise law.

## Why liquor licensing varies so much by state

Alcohol is listed as a state subject under India's constitutional division of powers, so each state legislature and its excise department independently set licensing categories, fee structures, permitted hours, and enforcement rules. A licence category and fee that applies in one state has no direct equivalent in another - some states use a straightforward annual fee model, others tie fees to outlet size, location, or alcohol type sold.

This means any business planning to open outlets in multiple states must budget separately for licensing in each state, both in terms of cost and timeline, rather than assuming a template that works everywhere.

## Common licence categories across states

While exact naming differs by state, most excise regimes distinguish between categories broadly similar to:

- **On-premises consumption licences** for restaurants, bars, and hotels where alcohol is served for consumption on site.
- **Off-premises retail licences** for shops selling sealed bottles for consumption elsewhere.
- **Event or occasional licences** for time-bound service at a specific event.
- **Manufacturing and wholesale licences** for producers and distributors, which sit outside typical restaurant operations.

A restaurant or bar will almost always need an on-premises consumption licence specific to its state.

## Dry states and dry days

A few Indian states maintain full or significant prohibition, meaning liquor licensing for standard restaurant service is not available at all, or is available only under very restricted conditions. Beyond fully dry states, most states observe **dry days** - specific dates (often tied to elections, national holidays, or state-specific observances) when liquor sale is suspended regardless of an otherwise valid licence. Any multi-state operator needs to track each state's dry-day calendar separately.

## State licensing reality check

| Factor | What Varies by State |
| --- | --- |
| Licensing authority | Each state's own excise department |
| Fee structure | Flat, tiered by outlet size, or location-based, depending on state |
| Prohibition status | Full, partial, or none |
| Dry days | State-specific calendar |
| Renewal cycle | Commonly annual, but confirm per state |
| Per-outlet requirement | Generally yes, but confirm local rules |

## Planning a multi-state or new liquor-serving outlet

Before opening a restaurant or bar with alcohol service, confirm the current licence category and fee for that specific state and city, since even city-level or municipal-zone rules can add another layer on top of state rules. Budget realistic processing time into your launch plan rather than assuming a fast turnaround, and keep FSSAI food licensing on a completely separate compliance track - passing one does nothing for the other, and both are independently enforceable.
