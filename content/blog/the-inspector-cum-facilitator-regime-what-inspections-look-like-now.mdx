The Labour Codes rename the traditional labour inspector as an **Inspector-cum-Facilitator**, adding an advisory role to the existing enforcement one - but how this plays out on the ground still depends heavily on each state's own rules.

## Key facts at a glance

- The Inspector-cum-Facilitator role combines **enforcement** with an explicit **advisory and facilitation** function.
- All four Labour Codes came into force nationally on **21 November 2025**.
- Final Central rules were expected around **April 2026**, following draft rules published **30-31 December 2025**.
- Labour is a **concurrent subject**, so each state must notify its own rules for the new inspection framework to be fully operational there.
- As of mid-2026, **most states have not yet fully notified** their rules.
- Inspectors retain the power to identify violations and initiate enforcement action - the facilitator role does not remove that authority.

## What changed conceptually

Under the earlier Act-by-Act system, labour inspectors were primarily enforcement officials who visited workplaces, checked records against a specific Act's requirements, and issued notices or initiated prosecution for violations. The Labour Codes reframe this role to explicitly include facilitation - meaning the inspector is also expected to guide employers, especially smaller ones, toward voluntary compliance rather than relying solely on punitive action after the fact. This is meant to shift the tone of inspections from purely adversarial to partly advisory, particularly useful for smaller employers navigating the Codes for the first time.

## How inspections are expected to be scheduled

A recurring theme across the Codes is a move toward more structured inspection scheduling - including web-based or risk-based selection - rather than inspections being purely at an individual inspector's discretion. The intent is to reduce arbitrary or repeated visits and make the process more predictable for employers. The precise mechanics of how inspections are scheduled and triggered depend on the rules each state notifies, so the exact process can differ from one state to another until rule notification catches up nationally.

## What has and has not changed in practice

| Aspect | Old regime | Inspector-cum-Facilitator regime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary framing | Enforcement-focused inspector | Enforcement plus advisory/facilitation role |
| Inspection trigger | Often discretionary | Moving toward structured/risk-based scheduling (state rules dependent) |
| Employer interaction | Largely reactive, post-violation | Includes proactive guidance toward compliance |
| Enforcement powers | Full powers to flag violations and initiate action | Retained - facilitation does not replace enforcement |
| Rollout status (mid-2026) | N/A | Depends on each state's rule notification; uneven nationally |

## Why this matters even before your state fully notifies rules

Even in states where the detailed inspection scheme has not been fully notified, the underlying Codes are already the law nationally as of 21 November 2025. That means the direction of travel - inspectors expected to advise as well as enforce - is the framework employers should plan around, even if the exact procedural mechanics in your state are still catching up. Employers who treat inspections purely as adversarial events risk missing the practical benefit of an inspector who may be willing to flag gaps informally before they become formal violations.

## How to prepare for an inspection under the new regime

Keep your statutory registers, wage records, and safety documentation current and easy to produce - this is true regardless of which regime is technically in effect, but it matters more now because a facilitator-minded inspector is more likely to engage constructively with an employer who is visibly organised. Where an inspector offers guidance on a compliance gap, treat it as a genuine opportunity to fix the issue before it escalates, since that is precisely the intent of the dual role. At the same time, do not assume the facilitator label means enforcement has softened - inspectors retain full authority to flag violations and pursue penalties where warranted.
