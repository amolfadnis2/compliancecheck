Auto dealerships in India carry a distinct compliance load compared to most retail businesses, combining vehicle sales regulation, insurance distribution rules under IRDAI, workshop safety and standard labour law. This checklist covers the areas that matter most for a dealership or multi-brand showroom.

## Key facts at a glance

- Dealerships that sell motor insurance need to register as a **Motor Insurance Service Provider (MISP)** under IRDAI guidelines.
- MISPs must retain records for at least **7 years** from policy issuance or appointment termination, whichever is later.
- A dealer can be sponsored by **one or more insurers, or one intermediary** - not both at once.
- **EPF** becomes mandatory once a dealership has **20 or more employees**, on wages up to **Rs 15,000/month**.
- **ESI** becomes mandatory once a dealership has **10 or more employees**, on wages up to **Rs 21,000/month**.
- A **POSH Internal Committee** is mandatory once a location has **10 or more employees**.
- Workshop operations typically need **Pollution Control Board consent** for painting, washing and effluent activities.

## MISP registration for insurance distribution

The **Motor Insurance Service Provider (MISP)** framework set by IRDAI allows an appointed automobile dealer to distribute and service motor insurance for the vehicles it sells, once appointed by an insurer or an insurance intermediary. This is separate from the vehicle sale itself and requires the dealer to follow a prescribed code of conduct covering disclosure, fair dealing and grievance handling.

A dealer's sponsorship structure matters: it can be appointed by **one or more insurers directly, or by one insurance intermediary**, but not by both at the same time. Dealers also need to retain insurance-related records for **at least 7 years** from the date of policy issuance or termination of the MISP appointment, whichever is later - a longer retention window than most general commercial record-keeping requirements.

## Dealer agreements and vehicle sale compliance

Beyond insurance, dealerships operate under manufacturer dealership agreements that set sales targets, territory rights and after-sales service obligations, alongside standard consumer protection requirements. Under the **Consumer Protection Act**, dealerships must honour warranty terms, disclose accurate vehicle information, and handle deficiency-of-service complaints, with escalation possible to consumer commissions.

## Workshop and service centre compliance

Most dealerships run an attached workshop or service centre, which brings additional obligations beyond the showroom floor: **Pollution Control Board consent** for painting booths, vehicle washing and effluent discharge; fire safety measures for areas handling fuel and lubricants; and worker safety provisions under state factory or shops and establishments rules depending on scale. These are typically inspected and renewed separately from the sales-side trade licence.

## Labour compliance across sales, service and back-office staff

Dealerships employ a mix of sales executives, service technicians and back-office staff, all of whom count toward the same statutory thresholds. **EPF** applies once a dealership crosses **20 employees** at the standard Rs 15,000/month wage ceiling, and **ESI** applies at **10 employees** on wages up to Rs 21,000/month. Multi-branch dealer groups should assess each branch's headcount carefully, since thresholds are generally applied per establishment in practice even though the legal entity may be the same.

## POSH across showroom and workshop environments

A **POSH Internal Committee** is mandatory once a dealership location has 10 or more employees, and this applies separately to each branch or showroom location rather than only at a head office. Given those mixed showroom and workshop environments often have different shift patterns and reporting lines, dealer groups should ensure the ICC and grievance redressal process is genuinely accessible to workshop staff, not just sales floor employees.
