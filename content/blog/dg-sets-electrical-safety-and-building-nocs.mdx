Diesel generator (DG) sets and electrical installations both need their own specific approvals in India - **pollution board consent and electrical inspectorate certification for the DG set**, and **periodic electrical safety certification** for the building - and both feed into the broader building NOC that keeps a premises legally occupiable.

## Key facts at a glance

- DG sets generally need **State Pollution Control Board consent** covering emissions and noise, separate from any building-level approval.
- Many states also require **electrical inspectorate approval** before a DG set can be commissioned and connected.
- Electrical installations in commercial buildings typically need **periodic re-certification** by the state Electrical Inspectorate, commonly on an **annual or biennial** cycle for larger loads.
- There is **no single national capacity threshold** for DG set exemptions - small backup units may get simplified treatment in some states, but this must be confirmed locally.
- A **building NOC** for occupancy or fire safety often requires DG set and electrical safety documents as supporting evidence.
- Responsibility for DG set compliance follows **ownership** - tenant-owned DG sets are the tenant's responsibility; building-owned DG sets are the landlord's.

## Why DG sets need their own approval trail

A DG set is a combustion source, so it falls under environmental regulation (air emissions, noise) independent of whatever building it sits in. It is also an electrical installation, so it separately falls under electrical safety regulation. Businesses often treat "we got permission to install the DG set" as one approval, when in practice it can be two or three separate approvals from different authorities.

### DG set approval components

| Approval | Issued by | Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pollution consent | State Pollution Control Board | Emissions, noise levels, stack height |
| Electrical commissioning approval | State Electrical Inspectorate | Safe installation, wiring, load capacity |
| Building/fire NOC linkage | Local fire department / municipal authority | DG set as part of overall building fire load and safety plan |

## Electrical safety: an ongoing obligation, not a one-time approval

Electrical installations in commercial and industrial premises are not approved once and forgotten. Most states require **periodic inspection and re-certification** by the state Electrical Inspectorate, especially for higher-load installations, high-rise buildings, or premises with significant machinery. This typically involves testing earthing, wiring integrity, and load capacity against what was originally approved, and issuing a fresh certificate.

Skipping renewal is a common gap precisely because electrical safety certification does not always have the same visibility as fire NOC renewal, even though inspectors treat it with similar seriousness.

## How this ties into the building NOC

A **building NOC** - the overall occupancy or fire safety clearance for a structure - is not issued in isolation. Authorities reviewing a building NOC application or renewal typically expect to see supporting documentation for major fire-load contributors within the building, and a DG set is one of the most common of these. An unauthorised or uncertified DG set, or lapsed electrical safety certification, can hold up an otherwise straightforward building NOC renewal.

## Who is responsible: landlord or tenant

Responsibility generally tracks ownership and operational control. If a tenant business installs and runs its own DG set for backup power, the tenant typically holds the pollution board consent and is responsible for ongoing compliance. If the DG set is part of the building's shared infrastructure - common in office parks and malls - the landlord or building management company usually holds the relevant approvals. Tenants should confirm in writing which category their arrangement falls into, since assumptions here are a frequent source of compliance gaps.

## Practical steps for businesses

Businesses running their own DG sets should confirm pollution board consent is current, keep electrical commissioning and periodic re-certification documents on file, and check with building management (in shared premises) that the building-level fire and occupancy NOC accounts for the DG set's presence and capacity.
