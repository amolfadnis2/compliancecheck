FSSAI licences and registrations issued from **1 April 2026** no longer carry a fixed expiry date - they stay valid until suspended, cancelled, or the business shuts down. This does not remove your other ongoing FSSAI obligations.

## Key facts at a glance

- The **FSSAI Licensing and Registration Amendment Regulations 2026** took effect on **11 March 2026**.
- From **1 April 2026**, new FSSAI licences and registrations have **no fixed expiry date**.
- Perpetual validity applies only to licences/registrations **issued from 1 April 2026 onward** - treatment of pre-existing ones should be confirmed with FSSAI.
- A perpetual licence stays valid until it is **suspended, cancelled, or the business closes**.
- The **Registration** turnover threshold was raised to **Rs 1.5 crore**.
- The **State Licence** turnover threshold was raised to **Rs 50 crore**.
- Annual returns and fee obligations continue regardless of the licence's validity period.

## What Actually Changed

Before this amendment, every FSSAI licence and registration carried a fixed validity period of 1 to 5 years, chosen and paid for at the time of application, after which the business had to file a renewal or risk operating on an expired licence. The 2026 amendment removes that fixed-term structure for new licences and registrations issued on or after 1 April 2026.

Instead of picking a validity period and expiry date, a food business now gets a licence or registration that simply continues to be valid as long as the business remains compliant and operational. There is no renewal deadline to track for these newly issued documents.

## What Perpetual Validity Does Not Change

Perpetual validity removes the *expiry* clock, not the compliance workload. Food businesses still need to:

- File **annual returns** as required under FSSAI rules.
- Pay applicable **fees** as they fall due.
- Maintain hygiene, labelling and safety standards on an ongoing basis.
- Display the licence number as required at business premises and on packaging.
- Cooperate with inspections, which have moved to a **risk-based** model rather than a fixed calendar.

A licence that never expires can still be **suspended or cancelled** if FSSAI finds violations during an inspection or audit. Perpetual validity is a change to the renewal mechanism, not a reduction in regulatory oversight.

## Who Is Affected

| Business situation | What applies |
| --- | --- |
| New FSSAI Registration/Licence applied for on or after 1 April 2026 | No fixed expiry - perpetual validity |
| Existing licence/registration issued before 1 April 2026 | Original fixed-term validity continues to apply unless FSSAI issues conversion guidance |
| Licence up for renewal around the transition date | Confirm with FSSAI or a licensing consultant whether the renewed licence falls under the new perpetual regime |
| Street vendor registered under the Street Vendors Act, 2014 | Deemed registered under FSSAI, subject to the same underlying compliance expectations |

## Should You Do Anything Right Now

If your current licence was issued before 1 April 2026, do not assume it has automatically become perpetual - the amendment applies to newly issued documents, and FSSAI's treatment of legacy licences at renewal time should be verified rather than assumed. If you are applying fresh or renewing after that date, you should end up with a licence that no longer needs a renewal filing on a fixed cycle, but you will still need to keep up with returns, fees and inspection readiness.
